# Round 9B — operational deployability of a set-level coordinator in a real LEO/NTN control plane
You are a systems architect familiar with 3GPP NTN (Rel-17/18), satellite payload control and O-RAN-style RIC designs. Context: the design in the attached contract deploys, at a 30.08 s decision cadence, a coordinator that scores complete association profiles over a bounded catalogue using a nominal joint-physics model (geometry, load, interference, ACM, per-slot PA energy), commits one jointly legal profile atomically, and falls back to a per-user baseline proposal when a 10 s compute budget is missed. Measured on one worker: catalogue construction 1.8–3 s, immediate scoring of ~1 000 configurations 1.7 s, three-offset continuation 18 s (being reduced), whole anchor 34 s.
Questions:
1. Where would this coordinator physically live (gNB/gateway, satellite payload, core, RIC xApp/rApp), and what is the realistic information it can have at decision time: user positions/ephemeris, per-beam load, interference estimates, activation state, neighbour proposals? Which of these are standardised, which are vendor-internal, which are simply unavailable? Cite the standard/interface where possible.
2. Timing: for a 30 s decision interval, what is a realistic budget split across measurement, transport, computation, validation, command delivery and action-effective time? Is a 10 s compute budget plausible? What does the standard say about measurement report periodicity and conditional handover execution that constrains this?
3. Atomic commitment of a multi-user profile: what mechanisms exist (conditional handover with multiple candidates, group handover, RRC reconfiguration batching) and what fails if commitment is not atomic?
4. The fallback executes an independently computed per-user baseline when the deadline is missed. What are the failure modes of that fallback in a real system (stale masks, partial application, oscillation), and what would a deployable design do instead?
5. What claim wording about deployability is defensible given that the study uses retrospective TLEs (nearest epoch, future epochs permitted) rather than causal ephemeris? What would have to change for an operational claim?
