# Round 9A — writing and positioning: a physics-successor change and a conditional claim, without overclaiming
You are a senior reviewer/editor for a wireless-communications journal (IEEE TWC / JSAC / TVT class). Context from the attached files: a multi-agent RL handover/association study for a LEO constellation originally used a non-standard power model in which per-beam RF power was anchored at the start of an association segment and reset on every change, so user load could not change energy; the study has been rebuilt on a standard model (per-user rate-target power control with a discrete ACM staircase, equal-airtime TDM, per-slot PA energy, activation costs). The learned policy has three value components: C1 immediate energy-efficiency surplus, C2 forecast/persistence, C3 coordination over simultaneous multi-user decisions. Development evidence (not results) shows joint moves are strongly sub-additive (collisions), an exhaustive unilateral best-response comparator is strong, and a nominal-model selector can lose to realised outcomes. The sealed plan pre-registers a conditional intersection-union claim on one primary configuration with a +0.5 % relative pooled-EE margin and QoS non-inferiority, plus a contingency ladder of pre-declared regimes.
Questions:
1. How should the paper present the model change? Options: (a) system model stated once as standard with the old model only in a deviation register, (b) an explicit "model correction" narrative with a before/after comparison, (c) two-model study. Which reads as honest and which reads as damage control to a referee? What must appear in the system-model section for (a) to be acceptable?
2. Chapter/section skeleton and the minimal figure/table set for a paper whose main result may be "C1 and C2 raise EE, C3 is an evaluated layer with a non-positive or conditional marginal". How do you present a conditional/negative component without the paper reading as a failed study?
3. Exact wording for a conditional intersection-union claim with a pre-registered margin, a TRAIN-only panel and a measured (not nominal) interval coverage of about 0.89. What must be stated next to every interval? What claims become unsupportable?
4. The contingency ladder pre-declares regimes (rate target, user density, activation cost). How should regime-conditional results be reported so that they are not read as post-hoc selection? What is the accepted vocabulary (pre-registration, sensitivity, exploratory) in this venue class?
5. What are the three most likely referee objections to this study as described, and the shortest defensible response to each?
