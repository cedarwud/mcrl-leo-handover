**The process fix is to require every critical acceptance test to demonstrate that it rejects a specific, valid implementation that violates its requirement.** “The intended implementation passes” is only half of the evidence. Mutation testing supplies the other half by deliberately introducing faults and checking whether the tests detect them. ([arXiv][1])

Your proposed indistinguishable pair is also the right idea—but it establishes **insufficiency of the reduced representation**, not universal sufficiency of the full representation. That distinction matters: otherwise the replacement test could acquire another claim it does not actually support.

On the facts you describe, the existing test is, at best, a training smoke test. It might detect a broken optimizer or an inability to fit its fixture. It does not demonstrate that pairwise coupling information reaches the model or that losing it makes the required distinction impossible.

Also, the absence of the word “coupling” is not itself decisive. A behavioral test need not mention implementation details. **The decisive question is whether a coupling-destroying implementation still passes.**

## 1. What is this failure called?

### The closest established concepts

**Pseudo-tested behavior** is particularly close. In the literature, a *pseudo-tested method* is executed by the test suite, yet removing its behavior causes no test to fail. Your case may involve a feature pathway rather than an entire method, so “pseudo-tested feature pathway” is a useful description, not the paper’s exact formal category. ([arXiv][2])

**Test-oracle inadequacy** describes the assertion problem: the test observes something, but not something that establishes the required correctness property. Related work on *checked coverage* explicitly distinguishes executing statements from having their results influence an oracle—an assertion or other correctness check. ([大學系統][3])

**Vacuous satisfaction** is another relevant concept. A specification can appear satisfied because some part of it plays no role in the successful result. Ball and Kupferman’s *Vacuity in Testing* studies precisely this problem, including mutations of specifications and systems to reveal it. 

There is not one universally adopted taxonomy covering every “green but uninformative” test. For auditing, I would use the following practical classification; these are overlapping failure mechanisms, not six mutually exclusive formal categories.

| Failure mechanism                | What makes the test uninformative                                         | Example relevant to your situation                                                               |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Logical vacuity**              | The assertion is true without exercising its intended condition.          | `all(...)` over an empty collection; a guarded assertion whose guard is never true.              |
| **Weak or wrong oracle**         | The assertion checks a weaker property than the requirement.              | Checking output shape or positive prediction instead of preservation of pairwise information.    |
| **Non-discriminating fixture**   | The inputs do not distinguish compliant and noncompliant implementations. | Every fixture labeled “synergy” remains predictable without coupling structure.                  |
| **Circular oracle**              | The expected answer inherits the same mistake as the implementation.      | Computing “expected” features with the same aggregation helper being tested.                     |
| **Disconnected execution path**  | The test bypasses the part where the requirement can be violated.         | Supplying a hand-built tensor directly to the model while production preprocessing collapses it. |
| **Shared specification mistake** | Implementation and tests agree on an incorrect interpretation.            | Both treat “pairwise terms” as “their sum,” despite the contract explicitly distinguishing them. |

A useful organizing framework is **RIPR**: *Reachability, Infection, Propagation, Revealability*. A test must reach the fault, execute it under conditions that create an incorrect state, let that state affect something observable, and check that observation with an adequate oracle. Ammann and Offutt use this framework to connect test inputs, execution setup, downstream execution, and expected results. 

Applied to your test:

> Did the test execute the real preprocessing? Did its fixture make scalarization lose a necessary distinction? Did that loss reach the observed model input or output? Did an assertion reject it?

A failure at any link explains why the test can remain green. This is more useful than asking whether it contains assertions or has high coverage.

## 2. How to test feature sufficiency—and what the pair actually proves

### The exact mathematical distinction

Let \(x\) be a complete admissible problem instance, \(f(x)\) its deterministic correct answer, and \(r(x)\) the representation available to the predictor.

The representation is sufficient for exact prediction on a domain \(\Omega\) precisely when

$$
\exists g\quad \forall x\in\Omega:\quad f(x)=g(r(x)).
$$

Equivalently,

$$
r(x)=r(x')\ \Longrightarrow\ f(x)=f(x')
\qquad\text{for all }x,x'\in\Omega.
$$

The equivalence is straightforward: a predictor must give the same answer to the same representation; conversely, when every representation-equivalence class has one correct answer, define \(g\) to return that answer.

Your construction finds a counterexample:

$$
r_{\text{reduced}}(x^+)=r_{\text{reduced}}(x^-),
\qquad
f(x^+)\ne f(x^-).
$$

**That is a proof that the reduced representation is insufficient. No training experiment is needed.**

For a balanced binary pair with opposite labels, a predictor with access only to that representation cannot exceed \(50\%\) expected accuracy on the pair. Independent randomization cannot improve that expectation. For two scalar targets \(y^+\) and \(y^-\), the best common prediction under average squared loss is their midpoint, with unavoidable loss

$$
\min_c\frac{(c-y^+)^2+(c-y^-)^2}{2}
=
\frac{(y^+-y^-)^2}{4}.
$$

These bounds follow directly from the shared input, rather than from an optimizer’s observed failure.

The proof method is an **indistinguishability argument**. “Representation-collision witness” is a clear descriptive name for the fixture. The same underlying reasoning—different structures being mapped to identical representations—is central to analyses of graph-neural-network expressivity. ([arXiv][4])

### But distinct full inputs prove less than you might think

If

$$
r_{\text{full}}(x^+)\ne r_{\text{full}}(x^-),
$$

then the information necessary to distinguish *this pair* has not been completely erased at that boundary. An unrestricted function can assign the two representations different answers.

That does **not** establish that:

* the actual model architecture can represent the needed mapping;
* its training procedure finds that mapping;
* the learned mapping generalizes;
* the full representation is sufficient everywhere.

These are different claims. In particular, supplying a graph or pairwise matrix does not automatically make an architecture able to distinguish every relevant structure; aggregation and architectural invariances can still erase distinctions. ([arXiv][4])

I would therefore split your acceptance requirement into four separate checks.

### A. Validate the witness using an independent scientific oracle

The two instances must be admissible under your actual problem definition, and their opposite answers must come from a justified reference calculation.

For an interaction-sign target, establish something like

$$
\Psi(x^+)>\epsilon,
\qquad
\Psi(x^-)<-\epsilon,
$$

where \(\epsilon\) exceeds the relevant numerical uncertainty.

A fixture called `synergy_case` is not an oracle. Nor is an arbitrary positive label. Suitable evidence could be a hand-derived special case, a small independently implemented reference solver, or frozen reference calculations with reviewed provenance.

“Independent” need not mean duplicating your entire simulator. It means that the reference for the property under test does not reuse the exact implementation decision that could be wrong.

**Do not invent opposite labels merely to make the contrastive test possible.** If the physics does not support the proposed pair, the pair is not evidence about the scientific mechanism.

### B. Establish ambiguity at the actual model boundary

Capture what the real production path passes to the encoder—not a separately constructed approximation of those inputs.

The comparison should cover the complete observable input: tensors, masks, resource identities, ordering information, global context, normalization results, auxiliary inputs, and any relevant state.

Your phrase “literally the same object” should mean **observationally identical**, not Python object identity. Two separately allocated tensors with identical contents can be indistinguishable to the model; making them the same mutable object is unnecessary and can introduce aliasing problems.

For a byte-level certificate, compare a canonical representation that includes shapes, dtypes, values, and relevant metadata. Exclude sample IDs or other side channels unless they are genuinely part of the intended deployment interface. For stateful predictors, the argument must include the relevant history and state, or reset them identically.

Most importantly:

> **Construct the pair so that the forbidden scalar summary is also identical.**

A pair that becomes ambiguous only when *all* coupling information is deleted may not catch your actual bug. A scalar-only encoder could still distinguish it if the two coupling totals differ.

Your decisive witness should satisfy

$$
r_{\text{other}}(x^+)=r_{\text{other}}(x^-),
$$

$$
s(C^+)=s(C^-),
$$

$$
C^+\ne C^-,
$$

while the reference answers differ. Here \(s\) is the particular scalarization you want to prohibit.

This tests the distinction the amendment actually demanded: **structure versus summary**, not merely “some coupling information versus none.”

### C. Verify preservation of the structured information

At the live encoder boundary, check that the coupling values remain associated with the correct resource pairs.

A shape check alone is insufficient: an implementation could broadcast the same scalar into every entry of a correctly shaped matrix.

Check selected or exhaustive small-case entries against an independent reference, including their endpoint associations and directionality where applicable. Then check that the full-context representations of the witness pair differ.

This test should fail under the historical scalarization bug without needing to train anything.

### D. Separately demonstrate architectural use and learning capability

To establish that the *actual architecture* can use the distinction, a strong positive control is a known parameter setting or a small fitted model that correctly separates both examples through the real preprocessing and model path.

A tiny fit is legitimate here—but label its claim correctly:

> “This architecture and training setup can fit this diagnostic distinction.”

It is not evidence of generalization. A held-out diagnostic family is needed for that stronger claim.

Also, asserting merely that predictions change when coupling changes is too weak. Arbitrarily wrong predictions can change. For the known witness pair, require the correct answers or signs with an explicit margin.

### Limits that should be written into the test contract

**A witness proves necessity relative to the remaining representation, not the unique necessity of one particular encoding.** Another representation could preserve the same information.

**Exact equality matters for the impossibility proof.** “Nearly identical” inputs are not indistinguishable to an unrestricted model. An approximate argument needs extra assumptions about precision, noise, or the predictor’s sensitivity.

**A pair does not prove population-wide sufficiency.** That requires an analytical argument, exhaustive verification over a genuinely bounded domain, or explicitly limited statistical evidence. Merely failing to find collisions is especially weak when continuous feature vectors are almost always unique.

**Stochastic labels require a defined estimand.** Two different random outcomes from the same state are not evidence of lost features. Decide whether the target is a realized outcome, conditional mean, or full predictive distribution.

**The requested collision may not exist on the admissible domain.** Other retained features might already determine the coupling structure. In that case, the structural contract can still be violated, but its claim that removal creates unavoidable ambiguity needs revision—not a fabricated fixture.

Metamorphic testing is useful for expanding valid witness families: it checks justified relations between multiple executions. For example, consistent resource relabeling may preserve an answer, while a particular independently verified reassignment of coupling values may change it. But a relation must be scientifically justified; “every coupling perturbation flips the label” is not a valid generic requirement. ([arXiv][5])

## 3. Would mutation testing have caught this?

**It could have exposed the test gap. A surviving feature-deletion mutant is exactly the warning mutation analysis is supposed to produce.**

The logic is:

$$
\text{deliberately violate requirement}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{run designated tests}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{no failure}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{missing detection capability}.
$$

However, there are two important qualifications.

First, a generic mutation tool may not generate “replace the pairwise representation with its sum.” Syntactic mutations such as changing arithmetic operators are not substitutes for a domain-specific architectural mutation.

Second, **mutation testing does not establish that the starting implementation is correct.** When the required behavior is already missing, mutating the remaining code may never ask the missing question. A faulty baseline can have excellent mutation scores against the wrong fault model.

For your case, I would maintain a small set of explicit semantic mutants:

| Deliberate violation                                                         | What the test should establish                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Replace structured coupling with the existing scalar summary.                | The full-context witness can no longer satisfy the required distinction. |
| Broadcast that scalar into the original matrix shape.                        | Correct dimensions do not substitute for correct pairwise values.        |
| Preserve values but assign them to the wrong resource pairs.                 | Endpoint associations matter, not just the multiset of numbers.          |
| Pass the block into the model interface but bypass its computational branch. | Acceptance checks actual use, not just argument presence.                |
| Replace the relevant predictor with a constant “synergy” output.             | Both sides of the contrast are checked against the oracle.               |

The endpoint-shuffling mutation must not be confused with a consistent global relabeling. One changes the scientific instance; the other may merely rename it.

### Count meaningful failures, not just red runs

Deleting an argument and causing a `KeyError` can make a mutation run red without demonstrating semantic protection.

That failure may protect an interface requirement, but it does not show that the test detects loss of pairwise information. Include **shape-preserving, runnable wrong implementations**, and record the assertion that rejects each one.

Similarly, confirm that the mutant actually ran. A wrong import path, cached feature file, cached checkpoint, or patched-but-unused helper can make a mutation appear to survive—or appear tested—without exercising the intended intervention.

### Practical cost

A useful planning approximation is

$$
T_{\text{audit}}
\approx
T_{\text{baseline}}
+
\sum_m T_{\text{tests relevant to mutant }m}
+
T_{\text{inspection}}.
$$

For illustration, 25 mutants running a four-second diagnostic subset require about 100 seconds of test execution before overhead. The same 25 mutants each requiring ten minutes of training require more than four hours. Those are arithmetic examples, not measured estimates for your repository.

The engineering objective is therefore to move structural and information-preservation checks **below the expensive training layer**.

Google’s published mutation-testing approach similarly emphasizes selective, incremental mutation and filtering unproductive mutants rather than repeatedly mutating an entire codebase. Its experience also highlights human inspection cost, not just execution time. ([arXiv][1])

### Lighter-weight approaches worth using first

For a one-day audit, I would start with **manual fault seeding**: perhaps 10–20 carefully chosen violations of high-consequence requirements. This targets your known risk more directly than thousands of automatically generated arithmetic mutants.

Next, try **extreme mutations** on critical helpers: return a constant, remove an effect, or bypass a branch. This is closely related to how pseudo-tested methods are detected. ([arXiv][2])

Use coverage to select relevant tests, and inspect whether the changed values influence their assertions. Checked coverage formalizes that latter question through dynamic slicing, although influencing an assertion still does not guarantee that the assertion is strong enough. ([大學系統][3])

For Python, `mutmut` supports incremental work, targeted runs, and coverage-based filtering. Its current documentation requires fork support; Windows use is through WSL. Treat it as an adjunct to your semantic mutants, not their replacement. ([Mutmut][6])

There is no defensible generic percentage of defects that these lighter methods will catch. Their advantage is that you can deliberately target the violations capable of invalidating an entire experimental interpretation.

## 4. Contract-to-test traceability: what discipline supplies the missing link?

The established discipline is **requirements-based verification with bidirectional traceability**, usually supported by a requirements traceability matrix and configuration control. NASA’s software-engineering guidance explicitly connects requirements to test procedures, test cases, test data, and verification evidence, and calls for maintaining those connections as requirements change. ([NASA SWEHB][7])

But a conventional matrix can still contain decorative links:

```text
REQ-COUPLING -> test_synergy -> PASS
```

That records an association. It does not establish that the test detects violation of the requirement.

For your project, extend each critical trace with a **fault witness**. I would require the following chain:

$$
\text{clause}
\rightarrow
\text{observable obligation}
\rightarrow
\text{oracle and fixture}
\rightarrow
\text{test}
\rightarrow
\text{violating implementation}
\rightarrow
\text{observed rejection}.
$$

For example:

| Illustrative obligation                                                   | Verification evidence                                                       | Deliberate violation that must be rejected                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pairwise coupling reaches the encoder with correct resource associations. | Live-boundary value and endpoint checks.                                    | Scalarization, broadcasting, endpoint mismatch.               |
| The reduced representation is ambiguous for the approved pair.            | Equality of all actual reduced inputs plus independently different targets. | Leakage of a case identifier or another distinguishing field. |
| The full architecture can separate the approved pair.                     | Correct predictions for both cases through the production path.             | Ignoring the coupling branch or returning a constant label.   |

Store the exact sealed-document version and clause location, the approved interpretation, test identity, fixture provenance, code/configuration version, mutant patch, and failure result. A hash establishes which document was used; it does not establish that its meaning was implemented.

### The acceptance rule I would adopt

> A critical requirement is not marked verified solely because its linked tests pass. Verification must identify the observable obligation and include a replayable, relevant nonconforming implementation rejected by the designated test. The rejection reason must correspond to the obligation being verified.

This is a process recommendation combining traceability and mutation analysis—not a claim that a finite mutant set proves all possible violations impossible.

Two additional boundaries matter.

**Trace clauses, not just documents or test names.** Words such as “only,” “not,” “before,” “after,” “same,” and “preserve” often introduce separate obligations.

**Do not force every prose statement into a unit test.** Some obligations need inspection, mathematical analysis, numerical validation, or distributional evaluation. An architectural requirement may legitimately need instrumentation. A scientific claim may need an experiment. Record the appropriate evidence type instead of attaching a weak test for bookkeeping.

Finally, test the tests against the **historical broken implementation**. The sequence should be:

> New test fails on the old bug; repaired implementation passes; replaying the old bug makes the designated assertion fail again.

That regression witness is substantially more informative than a fresh green test written after the repair.

## 5. The highest-yield one-day audit of your existing suite

I would not begin by reading a few hundred tests sequentially. **Audit the high-consequence claims first, then challenge their linked tests.** The following is an eight-hour allocation, not a promise that the entire suite becomes trustworthy in one day.

### Hour 1: establish that the test harness tells the truth

Freeze the code and configuration being audited. Run the baseline and record collection, skips, expected failures, and exit status.

Temporarily insert an unconditional failure into a few critical tests and verify that the real invocation reports failure. This catches harness or reporting problems. It proves only that the test is executed and failure is visible—not that the original assertion was useful.

Confirm that the tested package is the intended checkout and that production feature generation is not being bypassed by stale artifacts or mocks.

### Hour 2: select the critical obligations and formulate their opposites

Choose roughly the ten requirements whose violation could invalidate your conclusions: information preservation, target construction, masks, normalization, data separation, mechanism activation, and similar scientific dependencies.

For each, finish this sentence:

> “A plausible implementation that violates this clause would …”

Do this before reading the existing acceptance test in detail. That reduces the risk of accepting the test author’s interpretation by default.

An independent reviewer can derive these violations from the contract before seeing the implementation. A different person or model is not automatically independent evidence; the useful separation is in the **derivation of the obligation and counterexample**.

### Hours 3–5: run targeted semantic mutations

Apply one violation at a time and run the designated tests.

Record whether the mutant was executed, which assertion failed, and whether that failure actually expresses the contract. Preserve surviving mutants as concrete findings.

Prioritize tests with strong claims but weak observations: a scientific test name followed by shape checks; a “learns interaction” test using one label; assertions on training samples only; expected values generated by the same helper; or tests that construct model inputs manually.

These are triage clues, not automatic convictions. A small smoke test is legitimate when it is labeled and used as a smoke test.

### Hours 6–7: repair the most consequential gaps

Start with cheap deterministic checks: live-boundary feature preservation, valid collision witnesses, independent small-case target calculations, and bypass/constant-output mutants.

Do not immediately respond to a structural failure by launching another training experiment. First establish that the relevant information survives preprocessing and that the acceptance test rejects its destruction.

For each repair, replay the historical defect and at least one nearby shape-preserving variant. Otherwise the new test may reject only the most obvious implementation of the bug.

### Hour 8: replace undifferentiated “PASS” with evidence status

Produce a compact audit record containing the requirement, test, tested violation, execution confirmation, failure reason, and disposition.

Distinguish **verified against selected violations**, **surviving violation**, **not exercised**, **invalid mutation**, and **inconclusive because of nondeterminism**. Do not convert an unexamined requirement into PASS merely because the overall suite is green.

The day’s useful result is a bounded statement such as:

> “These critical obligations have demonstrated fault detection; these others remain unsupported.”

That is more valuable than another aggregate coverage percentage.

## What this means for the months of null results

Your diagnosis is plausible, but the strongest conclusion is conditional.

If you establish an admissible opposite-answer pair that the historical representation maps to identical model inputs, then **that representation could not express the required distinction**, regardless of training effort. That is a structural result.

It does not establish how often that ambiguity mattered in your experiments, that it explains every null result, or that the repaired model will learn successfully. The historical results remain observations about the implementation that actually ran—not automatically valid tests of the intended full-context mechanism.

The durable process change is therefore:

**Do not ask only, “What evidence makes this test pass?” Ask, “Which scientifically wrong implementation does this test demonstrably reject?”**

For every requirement capable of invalidating an experimental conclusion, retain that wrong implementation as a replayable test of the test itself.
