# Round 10D — Pre-mortem: assume the final result is wrong or the paper is rejected; what happened?
You are running a pre-mortem for a simulation study that is about to open its confirmatory runs. The attached files are the sealed design and two development reports. Do not review the design politely: assume that, twelve months from now, the study's headline conclusion is known to be wrong, or the paper was rejected after review. Work backwards.
1. Enumerate the ten most likely causes, ranked by probability × damage, each with: the mechanism, the earliest observable symptom, the cheapest test that would have caught it, and whether the attached documents already guard against it.
2. Separate them into: (a) physics/model errors, (b) target/label errors, (c) selection/optimisation artefacts, (d) learner/information errors, (e) statistical/inference errors, (f) execution/engineering errors, (g) framing/claim errors.
3. For the three most damaging, write the exact experiment or assertion that would falsify the concern before the confirmatory runs open, in a form that could be handed to an engineer today.
4. Name the assumptions in these documents that are stated as facts but are actually untested beliefs, and rank them by how much of the conclusion rests on each.
5. If you had to bet on the single reason this study fails, what is it, and what is the one thing you would do this week to reduce that risk?
End with: `VERDICT: TOP_RISK=<one> | TOP_THREE=<list> | CHEAPEST_TEST=<one> | UNTESTED_BELIEF=<one>`.
