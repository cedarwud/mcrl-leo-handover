# Controller strategic observation — the energy is in the control law, not in the association
Recorded 2026-09-10 after the owner said plainly that a one per cent improvement is not a meaningful result and asked for a direction that genuinely raises efficiency. This is **reasoning over today's measurements, not a new measurement**, and it is recorded as a direction to be decided, not started. `DIAGNOSTIC_NOT_CLAIM`.

## The arithmetic that settles where the leverage is
The power amplifier accounts for **94.8 %** of consumed power, and its draw is set by the **power and mode control law** — not by which user is attached to which beam.

| what moved | pooled efficiency |
|---|---|
| **correcting one defect in the control law** | **28.47 → 43.71, about +53 %** |
| everything the association layer can contend for — coordination, joint search, all three learned routes | **about +1 %** |

**The largest efficiency movement measured today came from a control-law repair, not from any association decision.** And that repair only removed a defect; nobody has asked whether the law is well designed.

## Why the association layer is limited to about one per cent
It is not a weakness of the algorithm. Association decisions simply do not move much energy. Two local optima of the association search differ by about eight per cent; the coordinated optimum sits about one per cent above the certified fixed point. Both are small because they rearrange *who attaches where* while the energy is spent on *how much power each beam radiates and in which mode*.

**The project has been looking for energy where the energy is not.**

## This was predicted this morning and I did not act on it
The strategic review's closing section asked whether the occupancy-induced blackout should exist at all, proposed comparing a service-aware power and mode policy against the existing law, and said: *if much of the coordination gain disappears, the contribution becomes a diagnosis of the control law and its remedy — possibly more useful than teaching three networks to compensate for it.*

**That condition came true.** Correcting the provisioning took the coordination gain from 6.36 % to about 1 %. I spent the remaining hours pursuing the one per cent.

## The option, stated with its costs
The three-route structure survives a change of decision object. Pointed at the control law rather than the association, the roles map directly: the surplus of changing a beam's power and mode setting; whether that setting persists; and the interaction between beams' settings. **The interaction is stronger there**, because power settings couple directly through interference rather than indirectly through shared airtime, so the third route would have more to predict, not less.

Three honest qualifications:
1. **The +53 % was a defect repair, not an algorithmic gain.** It demonstrates that the control law has high leverage — a single defect there cost more than the entire association layer can win — but it is **not** fifty-three per cent sitting available.
2. **The control law's improvable margin has never been measured**, exactly as the association layer's one per cent had never been measured before today.
3. **It is a large pivot**: targets, heads and three contract sections would be rewritten.

## What should happen before any decision
The screening run is already committed and its cost already spent. It answers whether the three-head architecture functions at all, and that knowledge transfers to any decision object.

Beside it, one measurement would answer the owner's question directly: **how far is the current power and mode control law from the best achievable, on the same panels?** That is the control-law analogue of the multi-start ceiling now being measured for the association layer, and nobody has ever asked it.

**Proposed, not started.** The owner was told the reason it is necessary rather than merely interesting, and the decision is theirs.
