Trigger: the provider fix pass rehearsal on a real 100-user world failed closed with `ProbeError: invalid power certificate for BASE:nearest-eligible` (17.8 s wall, 937 MB RSS) before any arm was evaluated. An infeasible or slowly converging BASE is a legitimate physical state, not an error; the runner must never stop on it.

1. **Solver contract.** The capped rate-target map p ← min(cap, Γ·(N₀W + I(p))/ĥ) with `force_cap` rows is a standard interference function (monotone, scalable), so the iteration from p = 0 converges to the unique capped fixed point. Iteration budget raised from 4 096 to 65 536; convergence when the max absolute change ≤ 1e-10 W **or** the max relative change ≤ 1e-9. Convergence is checked on the update, not on a separate residual that treats cap-bound rows differently.
2. **Certificates.** `CONVERGED` (tolerance met); `CONVERGED_SLOW` (budget reached, all changes over the last 1 000 iterations monotone and below 1e-6 W — recorded with the final change); `INVALID` only for non-finite values or a violated monotonicity check (a genuine implementation fault, which stops the unit). Cap-bound users are flagged `rate_target_infeasible`, transmit at cap, and are served iff their SINR after joint resolution ≥ SINR_min. A BASE with infeasible users is evaluated like any other profile.
3. **KATs.** (a) a 3-user fixture with spectral radius 0.999 converges within budget to the analytic fixed point; (b) a fixture where two users' targets are jointly unattainable certifies `CONVERGED` with both at cap and `rate_target_infeasible`; (c) an injected NaN yields `INVALID` and stops; (d) the real-world rehearsal on the quarantined world 1 evaluates BASE:nearest-eligible without error and reports the certificate distribution over anchors.
4. **Reporting.** Every receipt records the certificate per profile and the share of `CONVERGED_SLOW`; a high share is a physics finding to discuss, not a reason to change the model.
