## Verdict

**Do not stop on the proposed submodularity argument. The stated objective class is neither submodular nor supermodular in general as a function of coordinated reassignment moves.**

That is not merely a statement that the analysis is difficult. Below, I give a counterexample following your power, airtime, mode-selection, decoding, and energy equations in which:

* **Every unilateral reassignment is strictly harmful.**
* **A two-user reassignment strictly improves the objective.**
* **The same objective, on the same fixed set of proposed moves, has both positive and negative mixed differences.**

The hard threshold creates one identifiable source of complementarity, but it is **not the only one**. Coordinated exchanges and shared hardware activation costs already invalidate the general “congestion implies non-positive interaction” argument.

What remains parameter-dependent is whether **your frozen approximately 100-user instance**, with its actual quantiles, geometry, penalties, admissibility guards, and candidate restrictions, contains a useful improving coalition. The counterexample settles the universal impossibility question; it does not establish a C3 certificate for that particular instance.

## 1. Your proposed implication is correct—but only for the right set function

Fix the anchor \(a^0\), and fix **one proposed destination for each potential mover**. Define

$$
g(S)=F(a_S),\qquad S\subseteq A.
$$

The relevant submodularity condition is

$$
\Delta_{ij}g(S)
=
g(S\cup\{i,j\})-g(S\cup\{i\})-g(S\cup\{j\})+g(S)
\le 0
$$

for **every pair and every context \(S\)** excluding that pair. This is the standard discrete diminishing-returns condition. ([arXiv][1])

If this particular \(g\) is submodular, then your stopping argument works. Ordering the moves and telescoping gives

$$
g(A)-g(\varnothing)
\le
\sum_{i\in A}\left[g(\{i\})-g(\varnothing)\right]
=
\sum_{i\in A}d_i,
$$

and therefore

$$
\Psi_A\le0.
$$

At an anchor with every \(d_i\le0\), no such coalition can improve the objective.

**The problem is the premise—not that implication.** Your model does not generally make the induced move function \(g\) submodular.

There are also two important domain qualifications. Every singleton must use the **same destination it uses in the joint move**, rather than that user’s independently best destination. And the subset comparisons must be well-defined: if hard constraints make some intermediate assignments inadmissible, a theorem on a complete Boolean subset domain cannot simply be imported unchanged.

## 2. Even smooth congestion does not rule out complementary reassignments

The familiar theorem concerns adding elements to resources: a concave function of non-negative resource counts is submodular. But **a reassignment removes a user from one resource and adds it to another**. That change of coordinates does not preserve the same diminishing-returns orientation. ([arXiv][1])

Here is a minimal counterexample, without fading or thresholds.

Two users initially occupy separate beams. Let

$$
H(a)=\sum_u w_{u,a_u}-n_X(a)^2-n_Y(a)^2,
$$

where each user gets assignment reward \(0\) on its original beam and \(1/2\) on the other beam. Consider swapping them.

| Moves       | Occupancies | Objective |
| ----------- | ----------- | --------: |
| Neither     | \((1,1)\)   |    \(-2\) |
| First only  | \((0,2)\)   |  \(-3.5\) |
| Second only | \((2,0)\)   |  \(-3.5\) |
| Both        | \((1,1)\)   |    \(-1\) |

Both unilateral gains are \(-1.5\); the joint gain is \(+1\); and

$$
\Psi_{\{1,2\}}=-1-(-3.5)-(-3.5)+(-2)=4>0.
$$

Viewed as a function of **added assignment edges**, this is modular reward plus concave count rewards. Viewed as the specified **swap moves**, it has positive interaction.

More generally, for \(-\|n\|^2\), a move with occupancy direction

$$
z_i=e_{\mathrm{destination}(i)}-e_{\mathrm{source}(i)}
$$

has pair interaction

$$
\Delta_{ij}H=-2z_i^\top z_j.
$$

Opposite exchanges and same-destination arrivals can therefore have opposite signs.

This is why the more appropriate literature includes **exchange neighbourhoods, reassignment cycles, and discrete concavity**, not just “submodular congestion.” Local-to-global results in discrete convex analysis require specific exchange axioms and the corresponding neighbourhood; unilateral optimality alone is insufficient. ([arXiv][2])

A related caution: classic equal-resource association work by Ye and colleagues uses logarithmic utility, yielding load terms such as \(-n_b\log n_b\). That is a particular objective—not a general result about linear credited bits minus energy. ([arXiv][3])

## 3. A hard threshold can create complementarity, but does not always do so

Consider

$$
h(S)=C\,\mathbf1\{|S|\ge k\},\qquad C>0,
$$

with \(m\) possible helpful moves.

When \(k=1\), this is an OR/coverage function: the first sufficient move helps, and subsequent moves do not. It is submodular. When \(k=m\), it is an AND/unanimity function: all moves are necessary, creating supermodularity. For an interior threshold \(1<k<m\), it is **neither**: the marginal rises at activation and falls after saturation.

For example, a two-of-three threshold has a positive pair difference before activation and a negative pair difference when the third move is already present. This is a standard complementarity example; Chen, Teng, and Zhang explicitly discuss a valuation that becomes positive only when at least two items are obtained. ([arXiv][4])

### Your “two interferers jointly rescue a third user” intuition is valid under explicit conditions

Hold a victim’s transmitted mode, desired received signal, and airtime fixed. Its credited bits can be written

$$
B_v(S)=C\,\mathbf1\{I(S)\le L\},
\qquad
L=\frac{S_{\rm rx}}{\gamma_{\rm tx}}-N.
$$

Suppose the initial interference exceeds \(L\) by \(r>0\), and two moves reduce interference by \(\delta_i,\delta_j>0\). If

$$
\boxed{\max(\delta_i,\delta_j)<r\le\delta_i+\delta_j,}
$$

neither move alone rescues the victim, but both do. Its bit contributions across the four counterfactuals are

$$
(0,0,0,C),
$$

so its contribution to interaction is \(+C\).

If either move alone already rescues it, the pattern becomes

$$
(0,C,C,C),
$$

giving \(-C\) instead.

The full objective still requires

$$
\Psi_F=\Psi_B-\eta\Psi_E-\Psi_\Phi.
$$

A rescued victim supplies a positive component; it does not ensure that the total remains positive after donor losses, power changes, and penalties.

### The crucial qualification: your controller may cancel the supposed SINR improvement

For an **uncapped, exactly target-tracking** link,

$$
p=\Gamma(n)\frac{N+I}{h}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{SINR}_{\rm nominal}=\Gamma(n),
\qquad
\mathrm{SINR}_{\rm pred}=q\Gamma(n).
$$

With occupancy and \(q\) unchanged, reducing nominal interference lowers required power; it does **not** increase these SINRs.

Consequently, nominal interference relief cannot change the selected mode of such an uncapped link merely by reducing its denominator. Stronger candidates involve a cap-binding transmitter, a change in the cap-active set, or realised interference differing from the nominal denominator used by the controller.

**Do not construct an interference-rescue witness by freezing power when your actual evaluator re-solves it.** Association-dependent interference is explicitly treated as an endogenous complication in wireless association research, including the swapping formulation of Alizadeh and Vu. ([arXiv][5])

## 4. Your occupancy inversion has an exact explanation—and a useful numerical window

In the uncapped regime, your transmitted mode is

$$
m_{\rm tx}(n)
=
\arg\max_{m:\,\gamma_m\le q\Gamma(n)}c_m,
$$

with NO_MODE if no mode qualifies. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{NO\_MODE}\iff q\Gamma(n)<\gamma_{\min}.}
$$

If the singleton target is the lowest mode,

$$
\Gamma(1)=\gamma_{\min},
$$

then any \(q<1\) puts the prediction below the lowest available threshold.

This is **not primarily a realised decoding failure**. It is a mode-selection failure: no transmitted mode is selected. Your census should distinguish that from selecting a mode and then failing to decode it.

Increasing occupancy raises the nominal target and power in discrete steps. That can raise \(q\Gamma(n)\) above the lowest selectable threshold. The phenomenon is best described as **load-induced mode activation under the specified control policy**. I did not find a unique established name for this exact combination of target tracking, post-power margin selection, and NO_MODE behaviour. It should not be presented as a general physical law that congestion improves reception.

### The first concrete regime to check

Combining the package’s bandwidth,

$$
W=\frac{500}{3}\ \mathrm{MHz},
$$

its roll-off convention, and the lower DVB-S2 modes gives:

| Occupancy |      Required SE | Target mode | Ideal threshold from the table |
| --------: | ---------------: | ----------- | -----------------------------: |
|         1 | \(0.3\) bit/s/Hz | QPSK 1/4    |                   \(-2.35\) dB |
|         2 | \(0.6\) bit/s/Hz | QPSK 2/5    |                   \(-0.30\) dB |
|         3 | \(0.9\) bit/s/Hz | QPSK 3/5    |                    \(2.23\) dB |

These lower-mode values are present in the official DVB-S2 table. Its performance thresholds describe a specified quasi-error-free operating point; your deterministic decode step is a modelling abstraction, rather than a universal step rule mandated by the standard. ([ETSI][6])

For occupancies one and two to remain NO_MODE, while occupancy three becomes selectable, the condition is

$$
\frac{\gamma_{\min}}{\Gamma(3)}
\le q
<
\frac{\gamma_{\min}}{\Gamma(2)}.
$$

The common implementation-margin and roll-off shifts cancel in these ratios, giving

$$
\boxed{0.3483373150\le q<0.6237348355,}
$$

or equivalently,

$$
\boxed{2.05<M\le4.58\ \mathrm{dB},
\qquad M=-10\log_{10}q.}
$$

**This is an analytically specified two-arrival complementarity window.**

At an illustrative \(M=3\) dB, occupancies one and two select no mode, while occupancy three selects QPSK 1/3. If decoding succeeds, the beam delivers

$$
W\frac{0.656448}{1.2}
=
91.173333\ \mathrm{Mbit/s}
$$

in total.

The required qualifications are important: the resulting power must remain uncapped, realised decoding must succeed, and the remaining network effects must be included. The 3 dB value is an illustrative parameter—not a claim about your frozen elevation-specific quantiles.

More generally, calculate

$$
k_*=\min\{k\ge1:q\Gamma(n+k)\ge\gamma_{\min}\}.
$$

If \(k_*>2\), a pair-only search cannot find this particular activation. If no permitted occupancy satisfies the inequality, the mechanism is unavailable for that link even before checking its power cap.

### A separate inconsistency in the wording of the rate requirement

Your package’s v1.8 amendment, item 5, explicitly calls 50 Mbit/s a **power-control setpoint**, not a delivered-rate constraint. That distinction is necessary here.

Under the exact minimum-threshold target rule,

$$
\gamma_{m_{\rm tx}}
\le q\,\mathrm{SINR}_{\rm nominal}
<
\Gamma(n).
$$

Because \(\Gamma(n)\) was the smallest threshold among modes meeting the rate target, a selected mode below that threshold cannot meet the same rate target. In the example above, each of the three users gets approximately **30.39 Mbit/s**, not 50.

Thus the implemented score permits a successfully decoded user to miss the setpoint. A separate, genuinely higher PHY floor or other reserve can qualify that argument; the package’s tiny floor-rounding difference does not change the several-dB example.

**If 50 Mbit/s is intended as a literal hard requirement, the feasible problem needs to be distinguished from the current full-buffer score.** That would be a model-consistency or feasibility issue—not evidence of submodularity.

## 5. A counterexample for the entire objective, including unilateral optimality

To avoid relying only on an isolated bit component, I constructed and checked a three-user example using the stated physical accounting.

There are three orthogonal beams on one satellite. All users can use every beam. Channels are homogeneous, chosen so singleton RF power is \(0.1\) W. The reserve is the illustrative 3 dB above, and realised wanted fading equals one.

The physical constants are

$$
T=30.08\ {\rm s},\quad
p_{\rm cap}=1.65\ {\rm W},\quad
p_{\rm sat}=5.2178\ {\rm W},
$$

$$
\eta_{\rm PA}=0.35,\quad
P_{\rm chain}=0.2\ {\rm W},\quad
P_{\rm common}=0.338\ {\rm W}.
$$

At occupancies one, two, and three, RF power is approximately \(0.1000\), \(0.1603\), and \(0.2871\) W, so the cap never binds.

Equal airtime means the homogeneous beam’s PA energy is

$$
E_{{\rm PA},b}
=
T\frac{\sqrt{p(n_b)p_{\rm sat}}}{\eta_{\rm PA}},
$$

**not \(n_b\) times this amount**. Failed and NO_MODE users still incur energy.

I used the package’s reported reference energy price,

$$
\eta=19{,}720{,}681.00172232\ {\rm bit/J},
$$

and an explicitly **synthetic modular handover penalty** of \(1.2\times10^9\) equivalent bits per moved user. The synthetic gain, quantile, and penalty make this a counterexample to the model-class theorem—not a replay of the frozen scenario.

Start with one user per beam. Let the residents of Y and Z move to X.

| State             | Occupancies | Credited bits, Gbit |  Energy, J | Penalty, Gbit | \(F\), Gbit-equivalent |
| ----------------- | ----------- | ------------------: | ---------: | ------------: | ---------------------: |
| Anchor            | \((1,1,1)\) |                   0 | 214.455809 |             0 |          \(-4.229215\) |
| Either move alone | \((2,1,0)\) |                   0 | 162.884899 |           1.2 |          \(-4.412201\) |
| Both moves        | \((3,0,0)\) |            2.742494 | 121.367965 |           2.4 |          \(-2.050965\) |

Every one of the six possible unilateral moves has gain

$$
d_i=-0.182986533\ \mathrm{Gbit\text{-}equivalent}<0.
$$

But the packing pair has

$$
F(a_{\{i,j\}})-F(a^0)
=
+2.178249545\ \mathrm{Gbit\text{-}equivalent},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\Psi_{\{i,j\}}
=
+2.544222612\ \mathrm{Gbit\text{-}equivalent}.}
$$

Now include three proposed moves in one fixed move set:

$$
X\to Y,\qquad Y\to X,\qquad Z\to X.
$$

The latter two produce the positive packing interaction. The first two instead swap users, restoring occupancies \((1,1,1)\). Their interaction is

$$
\boxed{\Delta_{\rm swap}g(\varnothing)
=
-2.034026934\ \mathrm{Gbit\text{-}equivalent}.}
$$

**One fixed objective and one fixed move set have both signs. That establishes “neither.”**

The attached standard-library Python script checks all 27 assignments, all six unilateral alternatives, and the eight states of that move set. This result does not certify your QoS guards or a learned method; it conclusively refutes universal non-positive interaction under the stated scoring equations.

## 6. Energy also creates complementarity without any decode threshold

Suppose a beam contains \(k\ge2\) users, and all must leave before its fixed circuit power can be saved. For moves to already active destinations, that source’s circuit-saving contribution is

$$
v_{\rm circuit}(S)
=
\eta T P_{\rm chain}\,\mathbf1\{A\subseteq S\},
$$

where \(A\) contains all its original occupants.

That is a positive **unanimity-game** contribution: no proper subset saves the circuit cost; the complete coalition does. The same reasoning applies to the final users leaving an active satellite with a switchable common processing term. Unanimity games are a standard basis for coalition-value decompositions. ([arXiv][7])

The reverse shared-cost effect occurs when multiple movers open the same previously inactive beam. Their separate singleton counterfactuals each incur an opening charge; their joint assignment incurs it once.

Neither effect guarantees positive total interaction, because throughput and PA changes may outweigh it. But both are independent reasons that hard decoding is **not necessary** for complementarity.

The exact accounting is

$$
\Psi_F(\eta)=\Psi_B-\eta\Psi_E-\Psi_\Phi.
$$

For fixed assignments, interaction is affine in the energy price. A modular per-user penalty cancels from \(\Psi_\Phi\), although it still changes whether the coalition beats the anchor.

Also, the concavity of \(\sqrt p\) does not imply any particular curvature in assignment: power is endogenous, occupancy-dependent, capped, and interference-coupled.

## 7. What changes in the important special cases?

| Special case                                                          | Conclusion                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fully separable per-user rewards and costs, with no resource coupling | Modular: \(\Psi_A=0\).                                               |
| Addition-only resource allocation with discrete-concave count rewards | Submodular in those additions—not automatically in reassignments.    |
| Smooth congestion with coordinated exchanges                          | Either interaction sign is possible.                                 |
| Every user actually delivers an identical fixed number of bits        | Total bits are constant; the sign depends on \(-\eta E-\Phi\).       |
| Uncapped exact target tracking, fixed occupancy and \(q\)             | Nominal interference relief changes power, not predicted mode.       |
| Complete hardware evacuation or shared opening                        | Can create positive interaction, including higher-order interaction. |
| Expectation over fading                                               | Does not automatically restore submodularity.                        |

The expectation point is worth stressing. Averaging preserves submodularity **if every realised set function is submodular**. It supplies no such guarantee when that premise fails.

For instance, in a fixed-mode, fixed-power example with exponential wanted-link power fading, expected successful bits after interference reductions are proportional to

$$
\exp\!\left(c\sum_{i\in S}\delta_i\right),
\qquad c>0,
$$

which has positive mixed differences. This is an illustrative derivation, not an assertion that your Rician–lognormal model is exponential. In threshold-process literature, the need to check the distribution-composed activation function—rather than assuming random thresholds imply submodularity—is explicit. ([arXiv][8])

## 8. Where to search—and why singleton ranking cannot settle it

The most informative candidate groups follow **shared physical events**, not just singleton scores.

**Occupancy activation bundles.** Look for destinations whose actual \(q\Gamma(n)\) values cross a mode threshold only after several arrivals. Calculate the required bundle size and cap headroom first. The two-arrival window above is an exact example.

**Complete activation events.** Include complete beam evacuations, final-satellite evacuations under the declared hardware model, and arrivals sharing one opening cost. A four-user evacuation dividend is invisible to a neighbourhood capped at three movers.

**Swaps and cycles.** Include occupancy-preserving exchanges that avoid the temporary congestion or underloading caused by singleton moves. Their rationale is different from moving users independently toward spare capacity.

**Interference/cap boundary groups.** Look for victim links whose deficit can be covered by a small group of compatible interference changes, with the power controller fully re-solved.

These are mechanism-based search recommendations, not a claim that all complementarity is concentrated only at thresholds. The literature’s **supermodular degree** and **supermodular width** describe positive dependencies and bundle-dependent effects, including effects that appear only in nonempty contexts. Their algorithmic guarantees require additional assumptions and cannot simply be transplanted to your signed, non-monotone score. ([arXiv][4])

There is a particularly useful theorem-level warning about your ranking:

$$
g'(S)=g(S)-\sum_{i\in S}c_i
$$

changes every singleton gain by \(-c_i\), but changes **none** of the mixed differences or coalition residuals.

Therefore neither “most negative singleton” nor “least negative singleton” is universally the correct interaction ranking. An arbitrary modular handover price can change that ordering without changing complementarity at all.

For each candidate, decompose

$$
\Psi_F
=
\Psi_B
-\eta\Psi_{E_{\rm PA}}
-\eta\Psi_{E_{\rm chain}}
-\eta\Psi_{E_{\rm common}}
-\Psi_\Phi.
$$

That will distinguish genuine shared-cost savings, mode activation, realised decode rescue, airtime effects, and compensation for congestion. It also exposes positive results driven primarily by the current NO_MODE convention.

## 9. Your residual is standard—but is not purely second-order for larger coalitions

The established framework is the **Möbius transform**, or **Harsanyi-dividend decomposition**, of the anchored coalition game

$$
v(S)=F(a_S)-F(a^0).
$$

Define

$$
\mu(T)
=
\sum_{U\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|U|}v(U).
$$

Then

$$
v(A)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq A}\mu(T),
\qquad d_i=\mu(\{i\}),
$$

so your residual has the exact interpretation

$$
\boxed{
\Psi_A
=
\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq A\\|T|\ge2}}\mu(T).
}
$$

The unanimity-basis/Möbius representation is standard in cooperative-game mathematics. ([arXiv][7])

Thus:

**For two users**, \(\Psi\) is a second-order mixed difference.

**For three or more**, it is the aggregate of **all interaction orders above one**, not a pure pair effect and not a pure highest-order effect. For a triple,

$$
\mu(\{i,j,k\})
=
\Psi_{\{i,j,k\}}
-\Psi_{\{i,j\}}
-\Psi_{\{i,k\}}
-\Psi_{\{j,k\}}.
$$

A zero \(\Psi_A\) can hide cancellation between positive and negative interactions. Non-positive pair effects at the anchor also do not prove submodularity, because contextual differences depend on higher-order coefficients:

$$
\Delta_{ij}g(S)
=
\sum_{T\subseteq S}\mu(T\cup\{i,j\}).
$$

For attribution, Shapley or Shapley–Taylor methods are useful, but they answer a different question. In particular, subtracting the sum of ordinary Shapley values from coalition value would yield zero by efficiency—not a new interaction test. Order-two Shapley–Taylor can retain singleton effects and distribute higher-order contributions among pairs, but those pair attributions must not be called pure pairwise physical interactions. ([Proceedings of Machine Learning Research][9])

## What this means for your decision

**The universal structural negative result you proposed is unavailable: positive interaction is possible, including at a strict unilateral optimum.**

The next discriminating calculation is not another undirected expansion of a top-\(k\) neighbourhood. It is to check whether your **actual per-link quantiles, target modes, and cap headroom** admit the explicit activation condition

$$
q\Gamma(n)<\gamma_{\min},
\qquad
q\Gamma(n+1)<\gamma_{\min},
\qquad
q\Gamma(n+2)\ge\gamma_{\min},
$$

or another event-complete exchange or activation bundle.

A positive result must then clear three distinct hurdles: **positive interaction**, **positive net joint improvement**, and **admissible, decision-relevant benefit under the frozen evaluation contract**. None implies the next automatically.

Your weeks of empirical nulls may justify a negative result about the searched neighbourhood or the practical value of the mechanism. They do **not** justify reporting that the objective forbids positive interaction by construction.
