## 2. Detail and known-answer tests

### E1 — the reward and the endpoint disagree about handover, in sign and in size
The **per-step** quantities agree exactly: `_rewards` reads `physics["system_power_w"]` (`step.py:1068`), the endpoint reads `outcome.system_power_w` (`v023_physical_episode_runner.py:1018`); both come from `system_power_w(supply, beams_by_satellite)` at `step.py:993`, one supply draw per **beam** (`beam_power_w` = max over served users, `link_budget.py:439-464`), fixed term once per satellite. There is no separate handover energy anywhere: `HANDOVER_COST` is imported only inside `_rewards`.

So the disagreements are exactly four, and all are aggregation/pricing, not physics:
1. **Φ exists only in training.** The endpoint charges 0 J for a handover.
2. **The endpoint pays a *negative* handover cost.** `link_power[uid] = p⁰·G(θ_τ)/G(θ_t)` (`step.py:804-808`) is reset to p⁰ at every handover (segment dropped, `step.py:855-862`). At the frozen 3 dB in-segment budget the continuing link is at p_max = 1.65 W and the fresh one at 0.825 W. Measured with the repo's own helpers: supply 8.3833 W vs 5.9279 W ⇒ **2.455 W, 73.86 J per 30.08 s step per beam, = 8.75e9 λ-bits = 0.866 κ**. The endpoint therefore *rewards* the act the reward penalises.
3. **Aggregation** (E4): `Σ r1` is a sum of ratios.
4. **Calibration**: `r1/c₁` with `c₁` = 2.03e6 shrinks r1 by 347× relative to its raw scale while `r2/c₂` with `c₂` = 1.0 does not shrink r2 at all.

*Known-answer test.* Build a two-step, one-user fixture: user served on beam A at θ giving G(θ_τ); at step 2 the geometry drifts by exactly 3 dB. Arm H holds A, arm K hands over to an identical-geometry beam B on another satellite. Hand-compute (a) endpoint joules for both, (b) `Σ_t Σ_u r1` for both, (c) the scalarised reward. Expected: `EE(K) > EE(H)` by ≈73.9 J while `scalar(K) − scalar(H) = −0.30 + Δ(0.5·r1/c₁)`, i.e. **opposite signs**. Any implementation that does not show this has changed the model.

### E2 / E9 — how λ and κ were derived, and what a fixed-EIRP + ACM physics does to them
- **κ = 10 097 071 012.757404 bits** = `useful_bits / (steps × users)` (`ee_axis_calibration.py:142`), from the frozen receipt `steps = 10, users = 100, seed 2026082401, useful_bits = 1.0097071012757404e13, energy_j = 118 796.588` — one 10-step, 100-user Main window. κ is therefore "mean bits per decision on one seed".
- **λ₀ = 84 994 621.126** = `useful_bits / energy_j` on the same window (`ee_axis_calibration.py:141`). **λ′ = 118 424 222.855** replaced it on 2026-09-04 as the *pooled ratio-of-sums EE of the `BASE = Q1 + learned Q2` arm* over 12 rows of the V0.18 TRAIN analytic panel.
- Both are **operating-point EEs**, i.e. one Dinkelbach step, frozen and forbidden from iterating (repricing contract, "must not be iterated"). A one-step Dinkelbach parameter is only a valid surplus price when λ is already at the fixed point; off it, `Δbits − λΔE > 0` and `ΔEE > 0` differ exactly where `sign(EE_ref − λ)·sign(ΔE)` says they do — the 2026-08-31 finding, only *partially* addressed: the quantity is now the right *kind* (pooled ratio-of-sums, matching the endpoint), but it is the EE of a **different arm on a different world population** than stage C's `BASELINE`-anchored comparison.
- **Under fixed EIRP + ACM both constants change and must be re-derived.** λ is bits/J at the operating point: fixed EIRP removes the recurrence's power variation, so `P^N` becomes essentially `#beams × (const supply + circuit) + #sats × baseband`, and every joule is bought at a different rate; ACM changes the numerator's rate law. κ = bits/decision moves with the rate law alone. Neither survives a physics change: re-deriving is not "tuning against an outcome", it is the definition. **Nothing in the frozen contracts says so** — §7 lists "lambda" among the things that may not change after freeze, without the standing exception for a physics change.

*Known-answer test.* Take the frozen calibration receipt and recompute `useful/energy` and `useful/decisions`; they must reproduce `0x1.443a8f481639ap+26` and `0x1.2cea89d260f2ap+33` bit-for-bit. Then recompute the same two ratios on a fixed-EIRP replay of the identical window; the ratio λ_new/λ₀ is the factor by which every existing C1/C2 label is mis-priced.

### E3 — what C2 becomes with the recurrence removed (the central question)
Two distinct state surfaces carry the recurrence.

**(a) Q1's 228-D state** (`ee_axis_state.py:55-59`; 112 legacy + 4×28 context + 4 temporal):
| feature | today | under fixed EIRP |
|---|---|---|
| `maximum_required_link_power` block (28 dims, `:280`) | previous beam power / p_max ∈ (0,1] | **exactly `0.5 × beam_active_block`** — 28 dims perfectly collinear with an existing block |
| `previous_recurrence_power` (`:291`) | previous link power / p_max | **a constant on served rows, 0 on unserved** — degenerates to a served/unserved bit already implied by the incumbent block |
| `current_to_segment_start_gain_ratio` (`:335`) | G(θ_t)/G(θ_τ) | still computable, but it no longer *causes* anything; it becomes a pure geometry statistic (and under ACM, a rate proxy) |
| `segment_age` (`:314`) | steps since the segment started | still defined; its energy consequence vanishes entirely. Only a dwell/geometry proxy remains |
| `missing_incumbent` (`:321`) | incumbent absent from the candidate table | **unchanged and still meaningful** |

**(b) Q2's 448-D OPS-3 surface** (28 actions × 16 features, `ee_axis_ops3.py:529-568, 755-770`): 4 static (`load, power/p_max, beam_active, sat_active`) + 3 offsets × (`legal, χ_h, required_ratio, log_sinr`).
- `power/p_max` (static) → constant on active beams.
- `required_ratio = p̂/p_max` ×3 → **constant 0.5** on every legal row with positive gain.
- `χ_h` ×3 → loses its `~infeasible` (p̂ > p_max) branch entirely; survives only as D2-eligibility × cell-visibility, i.e. pure geometry.
So **7 of 16 features become constants** and 3 more lose half their content; the informative remainder is `legal`, `log_sinr`, `load`, `beam_active`, `sat_active` and a geometry-only χ.
- The **target** collapses harder. `terms = χ·Δt·(R − λ·ΔP) − (1−χ)·κ` (`:735-745`): with fixed EIRP, `ΔP` for an action on an already-active beam is **identically 0** (`:505-510`), and for a new beam it is a *constant* (one supply + one circuit, plus baseband if the satellite was dark). C2 stops being an energy quantity and becomes `χ·Δt·R − (1−χ)·κ` minus a constant beam-opening charge — i.e. a **rate-persistence** score, not an energy-persistence one. Its entire claim ("keeping a link alive is worth energy") is a statement about the recurrence, not about LEO forward links.

**Replacement features, if the recurrence goes.** The causal content that would remain is geometric and rate-based, and is not currently in the state in usable form: (i) predicted elevation / off-boresight angle **trajectory** over h = 1..3, not a gain ratio; (ii) predicted per-offset spectral efficiency or ACM MCS index (this is what actually varies under fixed EIRP); (iii) time-to-visibility-loss / remaining dwell of the incumbent beam; (iv) predicted beam **occupancy** at each offset (the bandwidth-share denominator, which is the real cost of joining a live beam); (v) a beam-opening indicator (new beam / newly active satellite), which is the only genuine energy step function left; (vi) the existing `missing_incumbent` and candidate-table churn. In short: C2 would have to be re-derived as *dwell-and-rate persistence with a beam-opening energy step*, and its λ-priced framing dropped.

*Known-answer test.* Rebuild one anchor's `EEAxisStateObservation` and one `OPS3Surface` with `recurrence_power_w` monkey-patched to return `p0_w` unconditionally, then compute the rank of the state matrix and the per-feature variance. Expected: `power_block − 0.5·beam_active_block ≡ 0`; `required_ratio` variance ≡ 0; Q2's action ordering must change on any anchor where the winner was decided by `required_ratio` or the p̂ feasibility branch.

### E4 / E5 / E2 — status of the three 2026-08-31 defects
1. **λ multiplier — PARTIALLY FIXED.** Repriced 2026-09-04 to a pooled ratio-of-sums EE (the right *kind* of quantity), but frozen at a non-iterated single Dinkelbach step, taken from a different arm on a different world population, and the **old value is still the library default** (E8).
2. **Per-head bootstrap — NOT FIXED, still live.** `modqn.py:542-550` bootstraps each head on its own `max`. It is defended as eq.-(16) fidelity (`:503-509`) and asserted by `tests/test_w08_vanilla_td_target.py`. Note the scope has narrowed: the successor's Q1/Q2 are **supervised pairwise regressions**, not TD (`algorithms/ee_axis_pairwise.py:222-231`, `residual = Q(s,a_c) − Q(s,a_r) − z/κ`, plus a `β·Q(s,a_r)²` gauge), so per-head bootstrap now affects **only** BASELINE. But BASELINE is the reference every stage-C claim is measured against, so the defect is now in the *comparator*, which is worse for interpretation, not better.
3. **r1 aggregation — NOT FIXED.** `step.py:1071` still divides each user's rate by the common `P^N`; summing over users and steps is a sum of ratios.

### E6 — mechanisms that make the learned BASE avoid handovers beyond Φ
Three, compounding:
- **Calibration asymmetry (E12).** `ω·|r|/c`: r1 ≈ 0.083, φ₁ = 0.15, φ₂ = 0.30, r3 (at 3) = 0.10. One inter-satellite handover erases ~3.6 served-steps of r1.
- **Free outage, expensive return (E6).** `classify_handover` returns `NONE` when the current step is unserved (`action_contract.py:447-448`) and `INTER_SATELLITE` on re-entry (`:451`). Meanwhile `r1 = 0`, `r3 = 0` for an unserved user. The outage step is a fully neutral transition; only coming back costs. The drop rules (`modqn.py:1260-1268`) additionally remove the empty-mask cases from replay entirely — `modqn.py:1250-1259` says so in as many words.
- **γ = 0.9 over 10 steps** (`trainer_spec.py:49`) means the discounted future EE a handover buys is at most ~6.5 steps of r1 ≈ 0.54, against an immediate −0.30 — the handover is barely worth taking even when the physics says it is worth 0.87 κ.

*Known-answer test.* On the frozen 9000-EP checkpoint, count handovers per episode and compare against the `beam_power_w` distribution: if the policy is Φ-averse rather than physics-aware, the served-beam power histogram should be pushed toward p_max (aged segments) relative to a random-legal policy on the same worlds.

### E7 / E14 — what C1 and C2 actually inject
- **Neither is decision-time.** Both are **training-time label sources**: pairwise `(state, a_ref, a_cand, target)` rows regressed by `update_c1`/`update_c2` (`.scratch/multi-catfish-v023-heterogeneous-trainer/v023_heterogeneous_trainer.py:239-345`). The "Catfish" injection is entirely a *supervised target*, with no replay, no bootstrap, and no masking of the target. At decision time the only mechanism is `masked argmax(Q1+Q2)` (`ee_axis_two_route_model.py:136-158`).
- **Ablation is source-side**, not head-removal: DROP_C1 trains Q1 on *neutral* labels for the same 100 updates (contract §2), so both arms have an equally-trained Q1 that is merely uninformed. This is the right design; note it means "DROP" never tests the head's presence, only its label content.
- **C1's target** (`ee_axis_opening_pairs.py:396-410` → `ee_surplus_targets.py:250-252`): `z₁ = Δt·(ΔR_focal − λ·ΔP^N_system)`. Focal bits only, **whole-system** energy. The non-focal rate externality `z₃` is computed and **discarded** for C1 rows.
- **C2's target** (`ee_axis_ops3.py:735-745`): `mean_h[ χ_h·Δt·(R_focal,h − λ·ΔP_h) − (1−χ_h)·κ ]`, centred at the reference action and divided by κ. Absolute focal rate, **marginal** system power.
- Adding a "focal-Δbits minus full-ΔP" score to an "absolute-rate minus marginal-ΔP" score with weight 1 each is the un-argued step. It is also why C2 systematically prefers loading live beams: `max(background, p̂)` gives ΔP = 0 while the whole Δt·R counts (`:505-510`), and the older/more drifted the incumbent, the larger the free headroom.

*Known-answer test.* One anchor, two candidate actions with identical focal rate: A opens a new beam on a dark satellite, B joins a beam already radiating at p_max. Hand-compute `ΔP_A = supply(p̂) + circuit + baseband`, `ΔP_B = 0`. C2 must prefer B by `λ·Δt·ΔP_A` ≈ 2.2e10 bits ≈ 2.2 κ regardless of any service consideration.

### E10 / E11 / E15 — masks and world hygiene
- Training masks the TD target with `-1e9` (`modqn.py:548`) and relies on `:1264-1268` to keep all-invalid successors out of replay; this is correct but makes the drop-rate a *gate*, not a diagnostic (see `runtime/outage_gate`).
- BASELINE deployment maps an empty mask to `NO_OP_ACTION` (`modqn.py:330-346`); the learned two-route deployment **raises** (`ee_axis_two_route_model.py:155-157`). In a matched four-arm ladder that is a per-arm difference in the failure mode, not just in the action.

The **V0.20 C1 repricing worlds `2026092001–2026092007`** are not: they are stage-C episodes k = 1401–1407, inside the 3000-episode falsifier window. The exposure is second-order (they fitted the d40 *source* checkpoint, not the successor's labels) and 0.23 % of episodes — declare it, do not rerun for it.

7. `SUCCESSOR-…-CONTRACT §9` binds the endpoint to "pooled from `TrainerEnvironment.last_outcome` (additive bits, additive positive energy, one ratio of sums)"; the *reward* is never described in the same units anywhere, so the two are never confronted.

## 4. Three assumptions to overturn first

1. **"Φ prices handover correctly."** Overturn: the endpoint charges 0 J for a handover and in fact *saves* up to 73.9 J per beam per step (0.87 κ in λ-bits) by resetting p to p⁰, while the calibrated reward charges −0.30 — 3.6× a served step's entire r1 contribution. Verdict **FIX**: reward and estimand must be made sign-consistent on handover before any further C1/C2/C3 result is interpretable.
2. **"C2 is a persistence/service-risk quantity independent of the power model."** Overturn: 7 of 16 Q2 features and 29 of Q1's 116 appended dims are functions of the recurrence and go constant or collinear without it, and the target reduces to `χ·Δt·R − (1−χ)κ` minus a constant beam-opening charge. Verdict **FIX (declare)**: C2 as built is a recurrence artefact and must be re-derived as dwell/rate persistence with a beam-opening energy step.
3. **"λ is frozen, so it is settled."** Overturn: λ is an operating-point EE (one un-iterated Dinkelbach step) taken from a different arm on a different world population, the superseded value is still the in-tree default (`ee_axis_ops3.py:66`), and a fixed-EIRP + ACM physics changes λ and κ *by definition*. Verdict **SENSITIVITY → FIX**: re-derive λ and κ under the adopted physics, delete the stale default, and record the λ-sensitivity of every existing C1/C2 sign.
