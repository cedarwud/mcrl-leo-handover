## Decisive end-to-end findings and known-answer fixtures

1. **Renewal premium.** Within a segment,
   \[
   p_t=p^0G^T_\tau/G^T_t,\qquad p_tG^T_t=p^0G^T_\tau.
   \]
   On any association change, `continuing=False` makes \(G^T_\tau=G^T_t\), hence \(p_t=p^0\) ([step.py:783](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/src/mcrl/env/step.py:783)). The reset also replaces the old received-signal invariant with \(p^0G^T_{\rm new}\). A policy may therefore select a new, larger gain and obtain both lower RF power and a larger wanted term. Episode warm-start logic does not repair within-episode renewals.

   **KA-1:** old \(G_\tau=1000,G_t=500\) gives \(p=1.65\) W and \(pG=825\). Handover at \(G_{\rm new}=2000\) gives \(p=0.825\) W and \(pG=1650\): half the RF power and twice the transmit-side wanted product without endpoint cost.

2. **The “3-dB derivation” is not a derivation.** \(p^0=p_{\max}/2\) makes the relative feasibility budget 3.010 dB, but nothing establishes that the cell’s spatial half-power contour should become a temporal, segment-relative budget. The implementation even permits segments starting outside that contour ([link_budget.py:242](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/src/mcrl/env/link_budget.py:242)). Also, the repeated claim that recurrence “only raises power” is false when current gain improves; the equation permits \(p<p^0\).

   **KA-2:** \(G_\tau/G_t=1/2\) returns 0.4125 W, disproving “only raises”; \(G_\tau/G_t=2\) returns exactly the 1.65-W ceiling.

3. **PA and beam-count scale.** At 0.825/1.65 W, exact code values are \(\xi=0.139172/0.196819\), PA draw 5.927900/8.383317 W, and PA+circuit 6.265900/8.721317 W. Forty beams therefore contribute 250.64–348.85 W before baseband.

   **KA-3:** three beams on two satellites give fixed power \(3(0.338)+2(0.200)=1.414\) W. A reset from the ceiling to \(p^0\) removes 2.455417 W of PA draw but no circuit/baseband charge.

4. **Interference fixture.** Active-set membership is the \(z\) gate; intra interference selects same-satellite, different-cell, same-colour terms, while inter selects every different-satellite same-colour term ([interference.py:384](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/src/mcrl/env/interference.py:384)).

   **KA-4:** for received terms `[wanted=10, same-sat-other-cell=2, other-sat-same-cell=3, off-colour=4]`, expected \(I_{\rm intra}=2\), \(I_{\rm inter}=3\), total 5; load changes alone must not alter it.

5. **Wanted-power inconsistency fixture.** The PA and interference paths use \(p_b=\max p_u\), but [step.py:951](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/src/mcrl/env/step.py:951) uses private \(p_u\).

   **KA-5:** two users on one beam require 0.825 and 1.65 W. The beam radiates 1.65 W. Under one continuous shared-beam waveform, both wanted terms must use 1.65 W; code gives the first user half that signal. Under per-user time-slot power, energy and interference must instead be time-weighted—also unlike current code.

6. **Rate and bits.** The code computes \(\gamma=W/(I+kTB)\), \(R=(B^w/U)\log_2(1+\gamma)\), and endpoint bits \(R\Delta t\). It has no traffic-demand cap and no handover-time deduction.

   **KA-6:** \(B^w=500/3\) MHz, \(\gamma=3\), \(U=4\) gives 83.333333 Mb/s and 2.506667 Gbit in 30.08 s; four identical users total 10.026667 Gbit regardless of load four versus four separate equal-SINR beams, while the latter consumes roughly four beam power blocks.

7. **Propagation/noise anchors.** The requested docs correctly state \(A(30^\circ)=0.5\) dB and \(T_{\rm sys}=242.294\) K ([LINK-BUDGET-NOTES.md:114](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/docs/LINK-BUDGET-NOTES.md:114), [LINK-BUDGET-NOTES.md:157](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/docs/LINK-BUDGET-NOTES.md:157)).

   **KA-7:** \(0.25/\sin30^\circ=0.5\) dB; \(kT_{\rm sys}(500{\rm MHz}/3)\) is −92.537 dBm. Rician power must have mean one. For dB-Gaussian shadowing, verify \(E[10^{-L_s/10}]=\exp[(\sigma\ln10/10)^2/2]\), not one.

8. **Instantaneous reward versus pooled endpoint.** Per-step \(\sum_u r_{1u}=R_t/P_t\) is exact ([energy_efficiency.py:114](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/src/mcrl/runtime/energy_efficiency.py:114)); pooled EE is the power-weighted mean of those step ratios, not their sum or arithmetic mean.

   **KA-8:** trajectories with powers `[1,9]` and step EEs `[2,0]` versus `[1,1]` have the same summed \(r_1=2\), but pooled EE is 0.2 versus 1.0. Thus the claim “reward energy term and endpoint joules are the same function” is **false over trajectories**. They share the same instantaneous \(P_t\), nothing stronger.

9. **\(\Phi\) asymmetry.** `HANDOVER_COST` enters only `r2_handover` ([step.py:1101](/home/sat/mcrl-leo-handover-e1/src/mcrl/env/step.py:1101)); endpoint totals only rate and system power. Nominal-physics optimisers and fixed-\(\lambda\) targets therefore see a free handover, while the legacy learned policy sees an unrelated dimensionless penalty.

   **KA-9:** hold/same-satellite-new-cell/new-satellite must return costs `0/0.5/1.0`; all three must produce identical endpoint energy when rates, beam powers, active beams, and active satellites are held fixed. That equality demonstrates the omission rather than validating it.

10. **Fixed-\(\lambda\) identity.** The surplus implementation correctly uses joules: \(\Delta E_t=\Delta t(P^C_t-P^R_t)\). Its decomposition cannot detect missing physical terms common to all branches.

   **KA-10:** with \(\lambda=2\), \(\Delta t=3\), rate-sum deltas `[+1,+1]` b/s and power deltas `[-1,+1]` W, step surpluses must be `[9,-3]` bits and window surplus 6 bits. The executed fixture passed.

## Magnitude of the known coordinator contrast

- Saving 3.226 W from a 260-W baseline is 1.241% of power; at unchanged bits it raises EE by \(260/(260-3.226)-1=\mathbf{1.256\%}\).
- Half a beam costs 3.133–4.361 W using the supplied 6.266–8.721-W bracket. The observed 3.226 W is therefore fully compatible with approximately 0.50 fewer active beams per step.
- Consequently, the power result alone does **not** identify successful interference coordination. Beam deactivation accounting can explain it without invoking a learned externality.
- Relative to a reported +2.9% EE, the equal-bits denominator benchmark is about 1.26 percentage points; the remainder must come from bits or nonlinear covariance. A formal decomposition must recompute both totals, not add percentages.
- A single fully aged-to-reset beam saves at most 2.455 W; the 3.226-W contrast therefore cannot be one reset alone, but may combine multiple renewals with beam activation changes.

## The three assumptions I would overturn first

1. **Replace the segment-anchored recurrence/reset rule.** Use a sourced forward-link power-control or fixed-EIRP model whose state does not acquire a free re-anchoring option at handover.
2. **Make signal, interference, and PA accounting use one coherent beam/time-sharing model.** Either all users see the physical beam power, or power/interference/energy are resolved by user time slot; the present max/private hybrid must go.
3. **Align training and evaluation.** Train on an additive fixed-reference EE-surplus or another objective whose trajectory ordering matches pooled ratio-of-sums, and include sourced handover useful-time loss; keep event energy as an explicitly bounded sensitivity until its system boundary is sourced.
