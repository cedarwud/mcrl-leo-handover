    12	## C1 — two different denominators were being called the same thing
    13	
    14	**Verified by reading both definitions.**
    15	
    16	| formula | name | value at 10 s, SEALED |
    17	|---|---|---|
    18	| `(EE_t - EE_base) / (EE_fixed - EE_base)` | **incremental prize** — the anytime receipt's definition | **42.9081%** |
    19	| `EE_t / EE_fixed` | **absolute fraction of fixed-point EE** — my acceleration pre-declaration and the scorer | **49.8155%** |
    20	
    21	**I have been quoting `42.91%` as "the fraction recovered" while my own axis is defined as the
    22	absolute fraction.** Those are different quantities and they differ by seven percentage points
    23	here.
    24	
    25	The `+100.7%` headroom is unaffected: it is `EE_fixed / EE_10 - 1` and is neither of the above.
    26	
    27	**Correction.** F1 and F8 state which formula they use, in the axis label. `42.91%` may never
    28	appear on an axis labelled absolute fraction. The scorer already computes the absolute form;
    29	the figure plan and the acceleration pre-declaration are amended to say so explicitly.
    30	
    31	## C2 — the two axes, as specified, are one axis
    32	
    33	**Derived from the scorer's own arithmetic.** Both axes reuse the same arm pools, and axis B
    34	divides by one fixed positive `EE_fixed`, so
    35	
    36	```
    37	fraction_FULL - fraction_DROP = (EE_FULL - EE_DROP) / EE_fixed
    38	```
    39	
    40	**Signs and ties must therefore match. No learned action is recomputed under a budget.**
    41	
    42	So "one run, two axes" — my amendment 1 — does not produce two measurements. It produces one
    43	measurement and a rescaling of it. **Reporting them as independent evidence would be double
    44	counting.**
    45	
    46	**Correction.** Until timed execution exists, the two are reported as **an offline EE contrast
    47	and its normalised display**, in one table, never as two axes. A genuine acceleration result
    48	requires arms to execute under the budget, with real cancellation and real fallback — which
    49	`SELECTOR-LATENCY-2026-09-10.md` shows the current path cannot do (median `12.014 s`, 65% miss
    50	rate, `_catalogue_with_census` at `8.299 s` median). **The acceleration axis is therefore not
    51	available from the first run and is not claimed from it.**
    52	
    53	## C3 — the continuation rule mishandles a non-monotone trajectory
    54	
    55	My rule equates monotone movement with non-convergence. A relative-contrast sequence
    56	`0, +2, -1, +3, -2%` is neither monotone nor flat; `0, 0, 0, +1, +1%` is a stepwise plateau;
    57	and strictly monotone changes of vanishing size are already practically flat.
    58	
    59	**Correction.** 500 remains the default stop under the existing "only if" rule. A moving but
    60	non-monotone trajectory is labelled **"unresolved at the planned horizon"** — neither converged
    61	nor unusable. Before any number exists, the following are fixed: strict versus non-strict
    62	monotonicity, a practical tolerance on the EE scale, the seed aggregate, the extension length
    63	and an absolute cap. **The rule remains blind to sign.**
    64	
    65	## C4 — "flat zero" says less than I claimed
    66	
    67	I wrote that a flat-zero contrast means the heads learned nothing usable. **It does not.**
    68	`FULL` and neutral can both improve identically (`10, 12, 14, 16, 18` for each) leaving the
    69	contrast at zero throughout; continuous score changes may never cross the catalogue's argmax
    70	boundary; opposing per-anchor effects may cancel in pooled `B/E`; or every arm may execute
    71	`a0` on a deadline miss.
    72	
    73	**Correction.** The reading becomes: **"no observed incremental pooled EE advantage on this
    74	fixed panel at the sampled checkpoints."** Before concluding anything about learning, report
    75	absolute per-arm trajectories, assignment turnover, per-anchor `B/E`, and executed fallbacks.
    76	**No inference from loss is permitted in either direction.**
    77	
    78	## C5 — EE and service can move in opposite directions, and I gave no joint reading
    79	
    80	Derived example: neutral delivers `100` bit at `10` J (`10` bit/J); `FULL` delivers `90` bit at
    81	`6` J (`15` bit/J). **EE rises 50% while delivered bits fall 10%.** Both statements are true.
    82	
    83	**Correction.** The signed EE contrast is retained and is called an **efficiency/service
    84	trade-off**, not an unconditional improvement. A breach of the existing service guard is an
    85	invalid deployment; a deterioration within the guard is a co-metric cost. **If no operational
    86	acceptance rule is stated before the outcome, overall preference is left explicitly
    87	unresolved** rather than decided afterwards.
    88	
    89	## C6 — the knockout requirement was stated too strongly
    90	
    91	Amendment 2 required decision-level separation between `ALL_NEUTRAL_CONTROL` and knockout
    92	before the primary number may be reported. **Separation is necessary for that label, not
    93	sufficient as causal validation** — and a well-fitted zero-target head becoming
    94	decision-identical to knockout is a plausible success of its own objective, not a defect.
    95	
    96	**Correction.** Keep the knockout label where equality holds. **Do not discard the trained
    97	contrast, and do not seek a deliberately worse neutral fit in order to manufacture
    98	separation.** Attribute the contrast to **informed-target versus exact-zero-target training,
    99	finite horizon included**, and report the deployed knockout separately.
   100

    28	## What I got wrong, and how often
    29	
    30	I have written **"the sealed head literals, unchanged"** into most worker prompts today. The
    31	head literals are **not sealed**. They are implementation defaults, and the file even
    32	contradicts itself: three per-route values plus a fourth default of `0.02`.
    33	
    34	I have also written that the **"sealed checkpoint format rejects more than 2,000 epochs"**, and
    35	used it to conclude that a 9,000-epoch protocol **"cannot be expressed"**. That bound is an
    36	implementation guard, not a sealed protocol. It can be changed by changing code, with the
    37	consequences that follow — it is not a scientific constraint.
    38	
    39	**This is the same error three times today in a different guise:** treating a question of
    40	**instrument validity or implementation** as a question of **scientific sealing**. The other
    41	two were the training gate G1 (which asked me to predict a measurement before permitting it)
    42	and the feasibility wall built on a figure I had myself recorded as not established.
    43	
    44	## The distinction I had been collapsing
    45	
    46	- **Scientific sealing** — the objective, the numerator, the fading-quantile scoring
    47	  convention, the service guard, the decision margin, the arm inventory's meaning. Changing
    48	  these requires a **declared policy change** with a rationale independent of any result.
    49	- **Compatibility pinning** — parameter shapes that a checkpoint schema digest depends on,
    50	  values that earlier receipts were produced under. Changing these requires a **new version**
    51	  and re-running affected comparisons. It is bookkeeping, not adjudication.
    52	- **Implementation defaults** — learning rate, activation, layer widths, cadences, caps.
    53	  Changing these requires only that the change be **declared before the measurement that
    54	  judges it**, and that the selection criterion be stated and outcome-independent.
    55	
    56	**A learning rate that diverges makes a measurement invalid.** Checking it is mandatory, not
    57	permitted. `LRSWEEP` was dispatched at 14:02 on that basis: seven points per route across three
    58	orders of magnitude, **selected by convergence only, with no EE quantity computed in that job
    59	at all.**
    60	
    61	## One genuinely sealed thing with an undefined term
    62	
    63	Contract v1 §F2 seals the **10 s** budget — *"on the declared worker count"*. **A grep finds
    64	no declaration of that worker count anywhere in the contract or any amendment.**
    65	
    66	`SELECTOR-LATENCY-2026-09-10.md` measured the deployed path on **one worker on a machine at
    67	load ~15**, and found median `12.014 s` with a 65% miss rate. **Against an undefined worker
    68	count, that measurement cannot be compared to the budget as a compliance verdict** — it
    69	establishes that one contended worker does not suffice, and that `_catalogue_with_census`
    70	(median `8.299 s`) dominates.
    71	
    72	**Owed:** the declared worker count must be named before any deadline-compliance claim is made
    73	in either direction. Until it is, latency results are reported as measured configurations, not
    74	as passes or failures against §F2.
    75	
    76	## What this does not license
    77	
    78	It does not license changing anything to make a result come out. Every one of the unsealed
    79	values above still obeys the same rule: **declare the change and its selection criterion before
    80	the measurement that judges it, and make the criterion independent of the outcome.** The
    81	difference is only in what kind of declaration is required, and from whom.
