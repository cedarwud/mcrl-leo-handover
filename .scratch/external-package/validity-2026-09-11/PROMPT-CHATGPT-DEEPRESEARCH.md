# Prompt for ChatGPT Deep Research — literature questions that do not depend on any pilot result

Context (read `README.md` in the attached package for the setting; the rest of the package is optional background): a LEO multi-beam satellite simulator; 100 users, 4 satellites × 7 beams (28 per-user actions), 10 decision steps of 30.08 s per episode; objective = pooled energy efficiency (Σ decoded bits under full-buffer Shannon capacity with per-beam bandwidth shared among the beam's users) ÷ (Σ consumed joules: PA supply power per lit beam + per-beam circuit power + per-satellite baseband, per-beam `max`/TDM accounting); the only action is each user's beam choice; handovers cost zero joules; a service floor (served fraction within −0.5 pp of the reference). A one-line hysteresis rule ("hold the incumbent unless the best challenger's nominal gain exceeds it by 2 dB") reaches ≈112 Mbit/J while a multi-objective DQN (MODQN, reward = 0.5·r1(EE) + 0.3·r2(handover penalty) + 0.2·r3(load), gamma 0.9, 9000 episodes) reaches ≈94 Mbit/J on the same episodes. A new "ratio learner" (three Q-heads for bits / energy / handover, greedy `argmax Q_B − eta·Q_E` with a Dinkelbach-style price `eta`, equal-share energy label `E_u = P_sys·dt/U`) and "catfish" demonstration replay (static pools of transitions from three scripted rules mixed into 15 of every 128 minibatch rows, raw rewards, no imitation loss) are being tested against it.

Research the following. For every claim give the citation (authors, venue, year, DOI or arXiv id), quote the relevant number or statement, and mark anything you could not verify. Prefer primary sources over surveys. Use at least two independent search framings per question and list the framings you used.

1. **Energy-efficient beam assignment / handover in LEO constellations.** In the published literature (2019–2026), which papers optimise energy efficiency (bits per joule, ratio-of-sums) via beam/user assignment or handover decisions with RL? For each: the exact EE definition (numerator: full-buffer vs demand-capped; denominator: radiated RF power only vs consumed power incl. PA efficiency and circuit power), the action space (assignment only vs power control vs beam sleep/on-off), the baselines used, and **whether any reports a centralised or oracle upper bound** and what fraction of it the learned policy reaches. Do any report that a simple hysteresis / max-gain rule matches or beats the learned policy on EE?
2. **Does handover cost energy in the literature?** What energy or overhead cost do LEO handover papers attach to a handover (signalling, re-synchronisation, beam-switching transients)? Is "zero joules per handover" a defensible modelling choice? Cite models that assign a cost and the magnitudes.
3. **Fractional (ratio) objectives in deep RL.** What is the state of the art for optimising a ratio of sums (Dinkelbach / parametric transformation) inside value-based deep RL, and what do papers say about updating the price `eta` only a few times per training run from the policy's own ratio? Any published convergence or failure results?
4. **Credit assignment for a system-level objective with per-user agents.** For independent per-user learners sharing one Q-network and a common system-level energy term (equal share), what does the multi-agent literature say about learnability (difference rewards, COMA, counterfactual baselines, "tragedy of the commons" results in independent Q-learning)? Are there LEO/cellular papers that solved beam sleeping or consolidation with a centralised master and per-user workers, and how much EE did coordination buy over greedy per-user rules?
5. **Demonstration / auxiliary-experience mechanisms in dense-reward, short-horizon problems.** DQfD (Hester et al. 2018), R2D3 (Paine et al. 2019), Competitive Experience Replay (Liu et al. ICLR 2019), JSRL (Uchendu et al. 2023), and any "catfish effect" RL papers (competitive replay between a main learner and a "catfish" learner): what do they report when rewards are dense and horizons short? What demonstration-to-agent replay ratios worked (R2D3 reports an optimum; give the number and setting)? Is there evidence that mixing scripted-policy transitions into replay **without** an imitation or margin loss helps DQN at all?
6. **The MODQN paper being reproduced** (a multi-objective DQN for multi-beam LEO handover with EE, handover and load objectives, weights 0.5/0.3/0.2, eq. 16 — identify the paper from the description and from `code/modqn.py` in the package if possible): what EE definition and power model does it use, what baselines does it compare against, and has any later paper reproduced or challenged its EE claims?
7. **The handover-rate budget** used as a constraint: Sun, Zhu & Peng 2024 set an inter-satellite handover-frequency budget of 0.004 per 0.2 s epoch; confirm the paper, the exact quantity and whether other papers use a comparable budget.

Deliverable: a structured report (English, with a Traditional Chinese executive summary) with a table per question (paper, year, venue, what it measured, the number, and the condition), and a final section "What this literature implies for the project's design": (a) is EE headroom above greedy/hysteresis rules typically found with assignment-only actions, or only with power control / beam sleep / traffic-aware scheduling; (b) is the demonstration-replay mechanism expected to help in this regime; (c) what problem formulation would give a learned method a real job.
