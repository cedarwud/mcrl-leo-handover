# Frozen facts for Opus Max: R3 held-out failure adjudication

Date: 2026-08-26. Read-only design review. Do not edit files, browse, run a
new model, tune against these held-out labels, or reinterpret a failed gate as
a pass.

## Goal and constraints

The desired training-time mechanism has three genuinely distinct Catfish
roles and no post-training auction, accept/reject coordinator, or access to
another user's simultaneous action:

1. R1 directly optimises system energy efficiency (EE).
2. R2 protects temporal continuity/exact stay and should affect EE through
   physically modelled handover interruption/energy.
3. R3 should manage a spatial beam mechanism that can causally affect EE while
   retaining an objective distinct from R1.

Effectiveness is more important than preserving three roles. A role must be
dropped or redesigned if evidence does not support it.

## Prior opportunity evidence

On ten development seeds, a deterministic focal-state-only proposal that
selects a high-SINR beam with zero previous demand produced a strict net +1
active-beam split, positive load relief, positive immediate system EE, and safe
service in 184/1,000 sampled states (18.4%, all ten seeds). Blindly applying
all such proposals reduced mean EE by 0.05377 Mbit/J, so an inactive-beam split
is an opportunity family, not a safe policy.

On ten newly held-out seeds, the same frozen proposal again yielded 1,000
eligible rows and 211 joint-positive rows (21.1%, all physics and labels
replayed). Always proposing had mean DeltaEE = -0.01557 Mbit/J and 0.2%
service-unsafe rate.

## Frozen state-sufficiency test

Before the held-out outcomes existed, the test fixed:

- exact current Q-head input only: 112 dimensions ordered as per-action
  `[access, log1p(SINR), theta, previous_load/100]` for 28 actions;
- proposal action indexes one output of the runtime-shaped
  `DQNNetwork(112,28,(100,50,50),tanh)`;
- target: strict split + positive load relief + positive immediate system EE +
  service safe, relative to the checkpoint's Q1-only reference action;
- fit seeds: first 8 development seeds; threshold calibration: last 2;
- three fixed network seeds, 300 epochs, fixed Adam settings and class weight;
- threshold: linear 80th percentile of calibration scores, labels not used to
  choose it;
- untouched held-out seeds: ten new seeds, 100 rows each;
- Q1 action/Q values, other-user state/action, reference/alternative outcomes,
  and labels were forbidden scoring inputs.

The result was independently rerun and verified. It failed:

- ensemble held-out AUROC 0.5498, required >=0.65;
- average precision 0.2499, required >=0.30; prevalence 0.211;
- proposal-SINR-only baseline AUROC 0.5946 and AP 0.2757, so the ensemble was
  worse than the simple ranking baseline;
- 294/1,000 accepted, 79 joint-positive: precision 0.2687, required >=0.3165;
- accepted mean DeltaEE +0.0307 Mbit/J, but seed-level descriptive t95 was
  [-0.1550,+0.2127] and only 7/10 seed means were positive;
- only 1/10 seeds met the per-seed precision/recall/coverage rule;
- safety and coverage engineering checks passed.

The three individual members were similarly weak (AUROC 0.5405--0.5551, AP
0.2481--0.2516). Calibration AUROC was 0.5351. This is not a threshold-only
failure.

## Structural facts to scrutinise

- The joint label depends on the Q1 reference action and realised system
  outcome, although those are deliberately unavailable to an independent R3
  head. The head need not beat Q1 on every realised sample to optimise a
  distinct expected-return objective.
- Each output action receives few supervised examples; the fixed proxy nearly
  interpolated fit rows but failed calibration and held-out generalisation.
- The state has candidate SINR and previous beam demand but no previous
  per-beam transmit power, beam spectral-efficiency composition, segment age,
  or other users' current intent. Adding current intent would violate head
  independence; adding lagged aggregate beam physics would not necessarily do
  so.
- Original R3 is `r3_u=-U_b`; summed over users it is
  `-sum_b U_b^2`. It balances congestion but does not price active-beam power.
- A proposed alternative is the decomposable activation-regularised potential

  `r3_u = -[U_b + lambda_on/U_b]` for served users,

  so `sum_u r3_u = -sum_b[U_b^2 + lambda_on I(U_b>0)]`.
  `lambda_on` would be fixed from an independent physical beam-activation
  power calibration, never tuned on the development or held-out labels. It
  creates a spatial congestion-versus-activation trade-off without directly
  copying throughput/EE into R3.

## Questions requiring one decision

1. What does the failed gate actually falsify: current state sufficiency,
   runtime-Q-head learnability, the Q1-relative joint label, or the independent
   R3 role itself? Separate verified fact from inference.
2. Is the activation-regularised potential above a coherent, independent R3
   mechanism with a causal denominator path to EE, or is it merely disguised
   R1 duplication? Check the per-user decomposition and simultaneous-action
   implications.
3. Choose exactly one primary next direction: minimally extend the independent
   state and retest, replace the gate with a separately frozen physical
   shadow-reward mediation test, or merge/drop R3. Do not give a menu.
4. Specify the smallest non-heavy, new-seed falsifier and numerical pass/fail
   criteria. It must not use a post-training coordinator or reuse these labels
   to tune anything.
5. State whether a defensible three-role design remains realistically possible
   now, and the maximum claim supported today.

Keep opportunity evidence, supervised separability, RL learnability, joint
composition, and long-horizon EE evidence explicitly separate. Give a concise
verdict suitable for controller adjudication.
