**Sensitivity convention.** Unless stated otherwise, each row changes that declared numeric quantity to ½/2 of its value, holding geometry, assignments and unrelated constants fixed, and propagating its intended algebraic dependants. For dB-valued quantities this means halving/doubling the **dB number**, not ±3.0103 dB; the distinction is explicit where material. Discrete counts may make a hypothetical input invalid. For a fixed trajectory, if a component contributes energy fraction `s`, multiplying that component by `f` gives `E′/E = 1 + (f−1)s` and `EE′/EE = 1/[1+(f−1)s]`, provided bits do not change. In an isolated, uncapped link at fixed target, `p ∝ N/g` and PA supply `∝ √p`; coupled links have no universal percentage bound. Modes, cap hits, outages, selection and trajectories can change discontinuously. Those caveats apply to every local sensitivity below.

Per-entry sensitivity, applying separately to **every row above**: halving/doubling `e` halves/doubles that mode’s credited bits while it remains selected, and changes its eligibility for the target. Changing that row’s `g` to `g/2` or `2g` multiplies its threshold by `10^(−g/20)` or `10^(g/10)`. This can remove/add the row to the selected set, change target RF and cause complete decode loss; unaffected rows remain available. Perturbing a MODCOD name/code-rate token alone is not a factor-two physical change: the numerical tuple owns the implemented rates. Table order is not monotone by threshold or efficiency, and the code correctly selects maximum efficiency among eligible modes.

The resulting advertised anchors are arithmetically correct: **28 modes**, maximum `4.453027/1.2 = 3.710855833 bit/s/Hz`, minimum threshold `−2.35+1.7−10log10(1.2) = −1.441812460476 dB`, and gamma-control target `7.528187539524 dB`. Maximum per-colour full-buffer rate is **618.475972 Mbit/s**. At a 50-Mbit/s setpoint, 12 users have an eligible target mode; 13 do not. A mode that meets the airtime target is not necessarily power-feasible, transmitted, or decoded. These four statements describe different tests.

**Formula 1 — amplifier supply and idle accounting.**

For supported radiated power `0 < p ≤ 1.65 W`, the engine uses

`S(p) = √(p psat)/ηmax`, with `psat = 5.217758139 W`, `ηmax = .35`, and `S(0)=0`.

Consequently `p/S(p) = .35√(p/psat)`: **19.6819% at 1.65 W**, **8.6676% at .32 W**, and 35% only at saturation. Saturated power is derived from the declared 5-dB cap back-off. The curve and efficiency are `DECLARED`, not validated by You or CPI. Calling .35 average or operating efficiency would change every PA energy figure. There is no second back-off reduction of radiated power. [energy.py:22][E22], [v1.8 §1][V18]

The primary model adds **no PA idle/bias term**. It adds .338 W of the separately defined chain circuitry and .200 W per active satellite’s processing increment; neither source defines those as PA bias. Thus the primary cannot represent an amplifier that remains biased as radiated power tends to zero. It represents ideal switch-off/zero bias by declaration.

The standby sensitivity instead uses `I=.698609768 W`: active chains cost `.338 + max(I,S(p))`; inactive chains cost `I` under the declared 12-chain census. It does **not** use `I+S(p)`. The floor engages below `p = 1.65/144 = .011458333 W`. At saturation the floor is inactive, so the PA still has .35 saturation efficiency. Adding separate non-PA circuitry legitimately lowers **system** efficiency without double-counting PA consumption. [energy.py:115][E115]

For comparison of meanings only, an additive PA bias would imply `ηsat,effective = psat/(psat/.35 + I) = .334333` for this I. If .35 already described total PA DC at saturation, adding I on top would double-count that part of the budget. That additive law is **not** what this code implements. Outside the supported range, exported `pa_efficiency` clips efficiency at .35 while `pa_supply_power_w` keeps its square-root law; they cease to be reciprocal above psat. All in-scope radiation caps lie below psat, so this does not affect supported engine operation.

**Formula 2 — energy identity and failed transmissions.**

For boundary `k`, TDM slots `j` have fractions `wkj ≥ 0`, summing to one. The boundary-average primary power is

`Pk = Σj wkj [Σb active (√(pbkj psat)/ηmax + .338) + .200 Ns,kj]`.

The time integral is `E = Σk (tk+1−tk)(Pk+Pk+1)/2`, with the scalar integration path separately handling declared event discontinuities. The standby path substitutes the floor/census expression above. This is a trapezoidal approximation across physical boundary times, while integration over the piecewise-constant **slot schedule itself is exact**. FDM sums simultaneous user RF within a beam before applying that beam’s one PA law. [energy.py:145][E145], [batch.py:483][B483], [integration.py:119][INT119]

The nonlinear order is correct: **evaluate PA power per slot, then weight by airtime**. Equal .825/1.65 W slots yield 7.155608836 W mean PA draw; PA evaluated at the mean RF would be 7.260165679 W, and at maximum RF 8.383317219 W. For the bare square-root law, Jensen’s inequality explains the direction. The same concavity argument cannot be applied globally after introducing a `max(idle,…)` floor.

`m_target=None` forces cap transmission when no table mode can meet the rate setpoint. Separately, `m_tx=None` or failed decoding gives zero bit credit but does not remove the attempt’s RF, interference, circuit charge or active-satellite processing. Scalar resolution builds energy from the original `slot.beam_rf_w`; batch energy uses `active & (power>0)`, **not** `served` or `has_tx_mode`. NULL/no allocated link is different and creates no active transmission. In the standby sensitivity its dark hardware can still consume idle energy. [architectures.py:716][AR716], [resolution.py:144][RES144], [batch.py:483][B483]

One cap-powered beam on one active satellite for 30.08 s costs:

`PA 252.170181940 + circuit 10.167040000 + processing 6.016000000 = 268.353221940 J`.

At this operating point PA is 93.9695% of modeled energy. At the recorded low-power .014244 W example, PA is only 59.1469%, explaining why the .338/.200 coefficients matter more under aggressive power reduction. These are component-share arithmetic examples, not measured network averages.

**Formula 3 — coupled capped fixed point and finite stopping.**

For one simultaneous slot configuration, let `gi>0` be nominal direct gain, `Ni>0` noise, `Cij≥0` cochannel gain including overlap/masks, `γi>0` the chosen target, and `ci>0` the cap. The implemented map is

`Ti(p) = min(ci, γi[Ni + Σj Cij pj]/gi)`;

forced-cap rows instead use `Ti(p)=ci`. TDM uses the full beam cap; FDM uses equal `1.65/nb` sub-caps and subband-consistent noise/interference. Power is solved on **nominal** gains; the fading quantile changes wanted-link predicted reception and transmitted-mode selection, not this power map. [architectures.py:361][AR361], [batch.py:304][B304], [v1.9 §§1–3][V19]

**Uniqueness is provable under these assumptions.** Write `b=γN/g>0`, `Aij=γiCij/gi≥0`. Starting from zero gives an increasing sequence bounded by the finite caps; continuity gives a fixed-point limit. For `a>1`, strict scalability `T(ap)<aT(p)` follows from positive b and positive caps, including forced-cap rows. Two distinct positive fixed points would contradict this inequality at a coordinate attaining their maximum component ratio. Thus there is a unique **capped** fixed point. This independently supplies the proof behind the [controller’s standard-interference assertion][SOLVE]; the code itself does not compute a conditioning or uniqueness certificate.

At cap, `γi(Ni+Ii)/gi > ci` means the desired target cannot be reached at that joint operating point. A unique clipped solution is not proof of uncapped SINR feasibility, and still less a delivered-rate guarantee. A user can be exactly at cap and meet its target at equality; “cap hit” and “infeasible” are not logically identical. A missing rate-target mode forces cap regardless of that placeholder target.

The exact implemented finite rules are:

1. Start at zero. Stop normally when maximum absolute **successive change** is ≤`1e−10 W` **or** maximum relative change is ≤`1e−9`.
2. At 65536 updates, if the last 1000 maximum changes are nonincreasing up to machine epsilon and all `<1e−6 W`, return `CONVERGED_SLOW`, which is **valid**.
3. Otherwise return `INVALID`; nonfinite values or a failed monotonicity check also invalidate. Thus exhaustion can invalidate even though the controller prose says “INVALID only” for nonfinite/nonmonotone faults.
4. Rate-target paths take one extra map update, multiply by `1+2e−9`, apply `nextafter(…, +∞)` and cap. The reported change/certificate is from the preceding loop; it is not recomputed for that final returned power.

Small changes do not uniformly bound distance to the solution. On an unchanged uncapped set F, the solution and its sensitivity contain `(I−AFF)⁻¹`; its norm can be arbitrarily large as coupling approaches criticality. A complete finite-step error bound is absent even for the normal certificate, and the slow branch permits much larger errors.

A **closed-form arithmetic counterexample, not a simulation or an observed project failure**, respects zero diagonal interference:

`A = [[0,.999999],[.999999,0]], b=(10⁻⁶,10⁻⁶), c=(1.65,1.65)`.

The unique answer is `(1,1) W`; the zero-start sequence is exactly `pn=(1−.999999ⁿ, 1−.999999ⁿ) W`. At n=65536, each component is **.063434701 W**, its last update **9.365662e−7 W**, and relative change **1.476426e−5**. Neither normal tolerance passes; the slow rule does pass. The solution error is **.936565299 W (93.6565%)**, and the PA power at the returned RF is only **25.1862%** of PA power at the true fixed point. The extra update/nudge does not repair this discrepancy. Halving/doubling the iteration cap gives .032236961/.122845441 W. This shows why a declared numerical tolerance must not be described as a certified physical accuracy bound. No assertion is made that existing runs entered this regime.

**Formula 4 — segment-anchored power reset.**
