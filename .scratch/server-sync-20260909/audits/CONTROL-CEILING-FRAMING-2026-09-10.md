**Fixed assignment is a useful isolation, with a conditional answer.** Let `J(A, π)` be pooled bits/joules for assignment `A` and a complete power/mode policy `π`, on the same physical workload and service contract. The question is:

`max_π J(A₀, π) − J(A₀, π₀)`.

That measures control headroom conditional on the assignment the pipeline committed. It does not measure all control headroom available after reassociation. Conversely, optimizing association under `π₀` measures association headroom conditional on that law. Holding a law fixed means rerunning that law for each assignment, including its power/interference solve; it does not mean retaining the old numeric powers.

Neither conditional gain is generally additive. Two separately useless changes can work together; two individually useful changes can recover the same waste. For example, efficiencies `J(A₀,π₀)=J(A₁,π₀)=J(A₀,π₁)=1` and `J(A₁,π₁)=2` give zero separate gains and a doubled joint result. With the three nonbaseline entries all equal to 2, adding the separate gains double-counts the improvement. These are illustrative counterexamples, not project measurements. Relative percentages also cannot simply be added along a sequential intervention.

The honest wording is: “On these committed assignments, under this information and service contract, the declared law leaves at least X recoverable by the evaluated control alternative.” Replace “at least” with a certified interval only when justified.
