# Why this is the decisive experiment

The project's owner requires three learned components, **each individually producing a substantial improvement** in pooled energy efficiency. An adjudication has just concluded that no decision object has yet demonstrated that, that universal impossibility is equally unestablished, and that the two-hundred-worker-hour panel must not be funded until this question is settled.

The set-level route predicts what is left after summing the per-user terms. **The question is whether enabling that residual changes decisions enough to matter** — measured as realised efficiency, not as prediction error and not as the residual's magnitude.

A separate fast look suggested cross-beam interference contributes only about two per cent. **That does not bound this residual**: a per-beam rule already sees a whole beam's users, and a sum of per-user terms need not capture shared activation costs, occupancy thresholds, or simultaneous moves.

# The experiment

**Eight preselected anchors**, cached where possible, under the **corrected margin provisioning**. **No exact-search prefix consumes the opportunity first**: build the catalogue directly, so joint improvements are still available rather than already taken by a classical local search.

**One bounded candidate catalogue per anchor**, containing at least: the incumbent; all legal singleton moves; shared-beam bundles and complete-beam evacuations; and **coarse joint power and mode alternatives**. State the generation rules and the cap.

Then, over that identical catalogue, compare exactly two selectors:

- **`ADDITIVE_ONLY`** — select by the **exact summed per-user terms**;
- **`WITH_RESIDUAL`** — select by **those identical terms plus the exact set residual**.

Everything else is held fixed: candidate coverage, observable information, service constraints, tie-breaking.

**Evaluation must be objective-aligned**: the realised 48-boundary committed endpoint, pooled totals, with a common ratio optimisation across anchors — **not** the fixed-price single-boundary snapshot that today's adjudication found is certified against a different objective from the one reported.

# What to report

1. **The pooled-efficiency gain from enabling the residual**, per anchor and pooled. **This is the decisive number.**
2. The same under a **demand-capped** numerator, `min(delivered, contracted rate)` per user, reported beside delivered capacity, since a companion job established that this distinction changes a headline by an order of magnitude here.
3. How often the two selectors commit **different** configurations, and the size distribution of the selected candidates.
4. Served counts **and** rate-target attainment, separately.
5. **Save the chosen power and mode controls and per-user delivery records**, with digests. An earlier fast look could not be rescored because it serialised only aggregates; do not repeat that.

# The screen, declared before the run

**Ten per cent pooled gain is the planning screen for "substantial."** It is a spend-authorisation threshold, not evidence about the system.

- **A large gain** establishes an opportunity worth learning and justifies proceeding to a paired source-contrast experiment.
- **A small gain stops this candidate design** — not every conceivable arrangement. **No small bounded search can honestly prove universal impossibility, and you must not claim it does.**
