## Method and timing contract

The legal nearest-eligible base assignment is the incumbent from time zero. Users and their legal options retain sealed deterministic order. Alternatives are evaluated in ordered microbatches of 16 using one persistent `StepEvaluator`; when a batch returns, the first strict `F = B - eta_ref E` improvement satisfying the unchanged baseline served-count guard is committed immediately and later alternatives for that user are not evaluated. Thus a checkpoint receives only a move whose evaluation completed by that timestamp, never a candidate credited with hindsight. The microbatch is the sealed dense evaluator's native vectorized path; scalar calls were piloted and rejected as an implementation defect after taking 176.84 seconds on the first anchor.

The stopwatch starts before base construction and `_legal_options`; it includes all configuration/candidate construction, physical selection evaluation, and cache misses. The immutable exogenous world tape is the input to a decision and was loaded once per world from the standing content-addressed cache; its load times remain in each receipt. The 48-boundary committed evaluation is run only after timing to measure the true action endpoint and cannot affect the search path. The final complete no-move sweep is executed only as a separate certification probe; its evaluations are excluded from the anytime and joint caches.

## Fixed-point change caused by first-improvement

Best-improvement completes each user's whole neighbourhood and takes its best move. First-improvement commits the first strict improvement in sealed option order and skips the rest of that user's neighbourhood. Both are strict monotone coordinate searches, but their move sequences enter different local basins. Here first-improvement is not merely a scheduling optimization: it changes every fixed point and happens to improve the pooled committed endpoint under both rules. The archived endpoints remain the exact 20/20 matched control.
