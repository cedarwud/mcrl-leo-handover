The traps below need a controller decision because they touch the engine protocol or a declared model choice:

1. **T7 — intra-satellite co-colour interference (protocol CHANGE, engine stage 4 + provider):** `PrimitiveCandidate` cross gains are keyed by physical aggressor **(NORAD, beam-chain)**, not by NORAD alone, so that co-colour beams of the same satellite are representable; the interference sum in `architectures.py` consumes the per-chain map; the provider emits per-chain cross gains (aggressor boresight = its own served cell, co-colour only). KAT: a fixture with two co-colour beams on one satellite serving neighbouring cells reproduces the legacy same-satellite interference term.
2. **T8 — 10° visibility floor:** stands as the successor's declared minimum operating elevation (`VERIFY_SOURCE`; a model choice, documented as a deviation from the legacy 0° screening). Because D2 entry elevations (≈ 19.7/23.4/27.7°) are higher, the floor affects only release/outage tails; parity KATs against the legacy at the decision instant compare the legal sets *after* applying the same floor to both sides and report the count of legacy-legal candidates below 10°.
3. **T11 — transmit-gain implementation parity:** the successor's pattern implementation is the model; the decision-instant parity KAT against the legacy uses a declared tolerance of 1e-6 relative on gain; a larger discrepancy is reported with the angle at which it occurs and decided before sealing (not silently accepted).
4. **T13 — user motion within a step:** users are held at their decision-instant position across the 48 forward boundaries (≤ 250 m of motion per 30.08 s step against ≈ 14 km cells) and moved between steps exactly as the legacy moves them; declared approximation, documented; the tape records the per-step user positions.
5. **T14 — TLE selection:** the legacy's nearest-in-time daily file rule with its max-age guard is inherited unchanged and recorded (file name + sha256) in every world manifest; a same-day rule is not introduced.
6. **T6 — `steps_per_episode`:** the world construction must be invariant to the horizon parameter (KAT: worlds built with 30 and 31 steps share the first 30 steps' digests).
7. **T9 — NaN/garbage from `require_all_healthy=False`:** the provider must fail closed (raise) on any non-finite primitive; never emit NaN.
8. **T12 — TRAIN/TEST self-check:** the assertion must be able to fail: KAT constructs a provider on a known TEST date and expects the raise; the split source (file and hash) is recorded in the manifest.
9. **T14 — tape scale (engine + provider CHANGE):** 100 users × 28 slots × 48 boundaries ≈ 134 400 candidate rows per step (≈ 4 M rows per 30-step world) cannot be materialised as Python objects or serialised as a 1.6 GB JSON. Boundary snapshots are produced as per-step numpy arrays (float64; identities as int arrays) and consumed by the engine directly; the dataclass views exist for KATs only. The world manifest digest is the sha256 over the generating inputs (TLE file names + sha256, world seed, layout, provider source sha256, rule constants) plus the canonical bytes of the k = 0 boundary of every step; full per-boundary arrays are recomputed inside a unit and never persisted. Determinism KAT: two constructions give identical manifest digests and identical k = 17 / k = 47 arrays for step 5.
10. **T6 — horizon invariance:** the provider always builds the 30-step universe (`shortlist_visible` duration = 30 × 30.08 s); a rehearsal on fewer steps truncates that universe and never rebuilds it with a shorter duration; KAT: the 3-step rehearsal world equals the first 3 steps of the 30-step world.
11. **T7 detail:** the same-satellite co-colour term carries the legacy P-10 peak-receive-gain override (eq. 3.12a); with per-chain keys the engine reproduces it; a KAT compares one victim's total interference (same-satellite + cross-satellite) against the legacy helper at the decision instant.
