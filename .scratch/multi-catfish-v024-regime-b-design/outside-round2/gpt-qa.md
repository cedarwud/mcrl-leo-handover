**Track B is defensible as a conditional mechanism study, but the registration is not ready to freeze. I would prioritize deployability in the original regime over the full Track B campaign.**

1. **Where I would accuse you of outcome-driven design**

The problem is not openly constructing a favorable regime. It is leaving enough procedural freedom to keep constructing until three positive contributions appear.

`01-TRACK-B-DESIGN-MEMO-ASTRA.md` §1 already provides the right disclosure: historical failures motivated B, selection uses development results, and conclusions are conditional on the selected regime. Keep that statement.

Three loopholes remain:

* **Repeated attempts.** `03-TRACK-B-PROTOCOL.md`, “Fast-iteration loop,” permits successive sealed iterations. Sealing each attempt does not make the overall search outcome-independent. Add:

  > “This registration covers one fixed grid and one campaign selection. A valid unfavorable outcome cannot trigger a revised scientific iteration under this registration; subsequent redesigns are disclosed as outcome-informed follow-up work.”

* **Selection after inspecting head marginals.** Protocol step 2 evaluates oracle marginals “per qualifying point,” potentially allowing fallback when the first point disappoints. Add:

  > “Select the first point satisfying Memo §4 before inspecting oracle head marginals. Subsequent oracle or trained-comparator gate failure cannot select another grid point; the affected campaign terminates.”

* **Conflicting authorities.** The Protocol names both proposals as binding, but `02-TRACK-B-PHYSICS-PROPOSAL-GEMINI.md` §§1–2 includes micro-sleep, changed circuit power, sum-power and a different grid. Add:

  > “The physics and grid in `01-TRACK-B-DESIGN-MEMO-ASTRA.md` §2 control; file 02 is background, not implementation authority. Only demand-capped delivered-bit accounting changes.”

Also resolve an operational contradiction: Memo §3 obtains λ_B/κ_B from newly trained MODQN, whereas Protocol step 2 occurs “before any training.” The initial probe also imports a demand guard referencing M12 before M12 exists. Freeze the dependency order and phase-specific references. Explain how “exact one-step targets” preserve C2’s specified H≤3 target.

2. **Thresholds and selection**

**Keep 5%, 1% and 0.5%.** They are defensible practical eligibility margins, not statistically validated constants or evidence of traffic realism. First qualifying G1→G2→G3 is preferable to maximizing observed gain, but it remains development-based selection.

I would tighten interpretation and implementation in `01-TRACK-B-DESIGN-MEMO-ASTRA.md` §4:

* **J₁−U₁:** Because the catalogs are nonnested, call this the *declared evacuation-catalog advantage over at-most-one-user edits*. It is not an unrestricted coordination premium.
* **Feasibility:** Specify identical service/demand requirements for the compared optima, including whether demand feasibility constrains optimization or rejects its resulting witness. The current service-only solve plus later demand guard is insufficiently explicit.
* **Interaction surplus:** For an evacuation S, define the signed quantity \(V(S)-\sum_{i\in S}V(\{i\})\), with frozen price, reference-bit denominator and aggregation. Include negative contributions. The pairwise Ψ definition alone does not specify arbitrary-size evacuation interactions.
* **World consistency:** Three of four positive worlds is a screening rule, not inferential confirmation. Conservatively, require the **joint-versus-unilateral contrast** to be positive in at least three worlds, using a prespecified breakdown of the pooled witnesses, rather than merely J versus reference.

Preserve the trained-FULL2 recheck and its no-fallback rule. That safeguard matters more than modest adjustments to the numerical thresholds.

3. **What should count as success?**

“Each component positive” is a legitimate **additional conjunctive claim**, but should not replace the architecture question.

The comparisons already specified in `01-TRACK-B-DESIGN-MEMO-ASTRA.md` §3 answer different questions:

| Comparison      | Supported interpretation                             |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| M123 versus M12 | Value of the specified additive third head           |
| S3 versus M12   | Value of the learned coordinator package             |
| S3 versus S0    | Additional value of learned configuration prediction |

For the owner’s criterion, `03-TRACK-B-PROTOCOL.md`, “Owner’s success criterion,” currently requires only positive estimates. Require a paired, world-cluster 95% CI lower bound above zero for **every** FULL–DROP contrast, alongside the service/demand guards.

Define FULL and DROP separately for each architecture. Frozen score removal and retraining without a mechanism estimate different contributions. For the coordinator, specify how dropping C1/C2 affects proposals, catalog membership and decoding. A frozen deletion result demonstrates conditional dependence on that component; it does not establish an independently beneficial training mechanism.

If only the coordinator works, require:

> “In the development-selected finite-demand regime, the set-level coordinator improved pooled EE over M12. The original additive third-head design was not validated. This result supports the redesigned configuration-level decision architecture, not rehabilitation of the original three-head method.”

If S3 does not outperform S0, do not attribute coordinator success to learned C3 prediction. This follows the claim boundary in Memo §7.

4. **Single most likely failure and cheap pre-check**

**The apparent coordination opportunity disappears against retrained M12.** Demand capping makes consolidation attractive, but C1 already prices complete network-energy changes and C2 contributes action-set information. Weak rule carriers may leave opportunities that those components learn to exploit themselves.

The appropriate pre-check is **already required** by `01-TRACK-B-DESIGN-MEMO-ASTRA.md` §4: repeat the selected-point headroom gates on freshly trained FULL2-B anchors, before the remaining C3 and full evaluation campaign. If they fail, stop without moving to another grid point.

This requires baseline training; it is not honestly a “before any training” check. Its value is avoiding the much larger remaining expenditure. The risk is explicitly acknowledged in Memo §7.

5. **Should Track B exist given E1?**

**Yes as a secondary constructed mechanism study; no as the immediate full-scale rescue campaign.**

`04-E1-RESULT-RECORD.md`, unnumbered result table, reports **U₁ +1.992% and J₁ +2.222%**. Their difference is only **0.230 percentage points of reference EE**. Thus, most reported headroom is already accessible within the unilateral oracle class. E1 establishes neither that joint coordination is necessary nor that its additional catalog advantage is practically recoverable.

Nevertheless, E1 removes the strongest justification for changing physics first: the original regime already contains physical opportunity. The unresolved issue is whether available information and the deployment interface can capture it—exactly the distinction in `06-GLOBAL-VIEW-ASTRA.md` §§5–6 and 9–10.

I would therefore **defer the 280–550 worker-hour Track B campaign** estimated in Memo §5 and prioritize the original-regime deployable coordinator question. Retain B only if its separate contribution is worth publishing even when additive C3 fails, one DROP marginal is nonpositive, or the strong baseline eliminates its headroom. Track A’s negative result remains the primary evidence.
