This is a records assessment, not an experiment. No learner checkpoint was read; no training, simulation, or physics computation was run. The enumeration boundary is every materially distinct idea in the supplied sibling records that changes the physical contract, observation, objective, training signal, action selection, or deployment boundary. ADR-001 through ADR-009 are nevertheless covered explicitly because the request names them; where an ADR is process-only, it is enumerated and rejected as such.

Evidence tags used below:

- **[V] verified by reading the record**: directly stated in the cited sibling file, or stated in the owner-supplied current-project facts and cited there as such.
- **[D] derived**: a necessary comparison or consequence of two verified statements.
- **[I] inferred**: a plausible transfer mechanism that would still need a project-local test.

The current-project facts F1–F9 are accepted as **[V-owner]** because they were supplied as today’s verified panel. They were not recomputed here. ## Lead B — DQfD, fact 8, and Q1 v3

### Is a demonstration loss the mechanism for F8?

**[V]** DQfD’s large-margin term acts on the same scalarized Q used by argmax and imposes

`Q(s,a_demo) >= max_{a!=a_demo} Q(s,a) + m`.

The sibling record verifies this mechanism and its limitation: any non-negative margin pins the demonstrated action as greedy and thus structurally caps that term at the demonstrator (`ref/docs/catfish-explainer-package/05-full-dqfd.md:176-238`). It also reports that the unscaled Atari margin was 1,884 times the sibling Q spread, while Q-scale adaptation reduced the loss imbalance without removing the structural imitation constraint (`:117-172`). **[V]** On the historical load-only contract it was **“tried and failed”**: ON averaged 440.63 versus OFF 523.78, paired −83.15, worse in 6/6 seeds (`ref/docs/dqfd-env-viability-dr-package-2026-07-18/02-MEASUREMENTS-LEDGER.md:6-15`). Those old artifacts were later ruled out for corrected-physics claims (`ref/docs/research/env-foundation/HANDOFF-full-angle-aware-ee-closure-2026-08-07.md:12-17`), so the failure reason is not assumed to transfer.

**[D] Yes, with a precise scope.** For F8, a supervised demonstration-ranking loss is the direct mechanism that can force the learned ordering toward `RSS_MAX` on demonstrated states. It addresses the observed “every differing learned choice has lower nominal gain” more directly than generic replay injection. The demonstrator is unusually attractive here: it is cheap, deterministic, deployable, full-service, and uses less information than the learner.

**[D]** But it does not repair missing information. If Q1 v2 receives no feature from which two otherwise similar options’ nominal gains can be distinguished, identical decision inputs cannot be trained to two different RSS-ranked outputs consistently. DQfD can memorize correlations or state-specific aliases; it cannot make an absent variable present.

### Does Q1 v3 make DQfD redundant?

**No. They are complementary, in this order:**

1. **Q1 v3 feature addition is necessary representation work.** It makes RSS ordering expressible by placing `decision_boundary_log_nominal_gain_db` at the decision boundary.
2. **DQfD is optional optimization guidance.** It says which expressible action should outrank the others on demonstration states and supplies a strong starting policy.

**[D]** The feature addition may make DQfD unnecessary empirically—plain linear or TD learning might now recover RSS_MAX by itself—but it does not make the mechanisms logically redundant. That is an empirical question for a future matched comparison, not answered here.

**[D]** There is also a ceiling conflict with F7: RSS_MAX is 10.78% below the jointly constructed profile. A permanent positive margin can anchor the learner at the per-user demonstrator exactly where joint coordination could do better. Therefore DQfD is best interpreted as a warm-start/ranking anchor and must be compared with margin-free demo TD; it must not be called the route to exceed the demonstrator. The sibling itself made this distinction and required the margin-off arm to answer whether learning could beat a lookup (`ref/docs/catfish-explainer-package/05-full-dqfd.md:225-246`, `:326-354`).

## Part 3 — ranked transfer shortlist (maximum five)

### 1. Optimize an additive system-EE contribution that closes exactly to pooled EE

- **Binding released:** F2, while respecting F1’s measured preference for concentration.
- **Why it transfers:** the algebraic identity `sum_u r_u = system EE` and ratio-of-sums reporting do not depend on the sibling’s channel numbers. The physics provider supplies rate and power; the closure governs how they enter learning and evaluation.
- **Sealed-inventory change:** replace or add an objective route whose per-user terms add to the same pooled system EE used for judgment; keep exact-`F` as a diagnostic until order equivalence is demonstrated. Do not add fairness/spread pressure merely because the sibling did.
- **Cost:** medium. It touches reward definition, calibration, checkpoint selection semantics, and every consumer of the objective, so it requires a new versioned route even though the formula is simple.

### 2. Add RSS_MAX demonstrations with a DQfD-style ranking loss—only on Q1 v3 or later

- **Binding released:** F8.
- **Why it transfers:** the demonstrator is project-local and already measured; only the mechanism transfers. The sibling’s data and physics are irrelevant.
- **Sealed-inventory change:** a demonstration dataset carrying the same sealed features plus RSS_MAX action labels; a demo-aware replay partition; Q-scale-aware large-margin ranking on demonstration rows; optional pretraining; and an explicit margin-free demo-TD control. The Q1 v3 nominal-gain feature stays—DQfD does not replace it.
- **Cost:** medium. Data production is cheap, but loss scaling, action-mask parity, leakage control, and the F7 demonstrator ceiling require careful preregistration. Because F9 favors a plain linear head, Q1 v3 plus linear supervised ranking is the cheaper first gate; the full replay/pretrain/L2 bundle is justified only if that gate fails. The sibling bundle was **tried and failed** on an obsolete load-only contract; that result is a caution, not a current-physics veto.

### 3. Add a joint-profile construction route, preferably deterministic before learned

- **Binding released:** F5 and F7; F6 makes exact/local combinatorial construction unusually tractable.
- **Why it transfers:** F7 is project-local proof that independent rules leave 10.78% on the table. Joint optimization addresses the decision factorization, not a satellite propagation constant.
- **Sealed-inventory change:** retain the per-user scores but add a profile-level consumer that evaluates/constructs assignments jointly. Start with the already-demonstrated non-learned auction/assignment family as a diagnostic ceiling; a learned coordination decoder is a separate, higher-cost hypothesis whose faithful-environment reachability remained unresolved in the sibling.
- **Cost:** medium for a deterministic bounded-catalog solver; high for a learned decoder. It also changes the deployment boundary, so it is disallowed if pure per-user argmax is a project requirement.

### 4. Replace the single interaction scalar with per-option congestion context

- **Binding released:** F5.
- **Why it transfers:** the exact congestion equation differs, but the structural issue does not: a scalar added once cannot represent which candidate option caused which occupancy-dependent spectral-efficiency/power consequence. The sibling’s `χ` pattern is an example of pre-action, option-indexed context rather than a transferable numerical feature set. Its 0.4% sibling result tested `χ` as a de-collapse device; F3 says that is not this project’s problem, while F5 identifies a different, directly matching information bottleneck.
- **Sealed-inventory change:** add option-aligned occupancy/load context at the same boundary as Q1 v3’s nominal gain. Do **not** import the sibling’s cap-specific “competitor rank” blindly; derive only fields supported by this project’s `required_se(rate, occupancy)` and 1.65 W cap.
- **Cost:** medium. The representation and linear-calibration tests change, but no new optimizer or deployment coordinator is required.
