## 2. Flaws and Overclaims in the Audit Outside the 14 Checks

1. **Conflating throughput degradation with service failure (§A.2.2):**
   The audit writes: *"B1 delivers 43% less service for 7% less EE, and a better packing could do the same at zero EE cost... referee will ask why an policy that halves everyone's rate counts as a win."*
   - *Flaw:* B1 serves **99.81%** of user-steps (virtually identical to A m=2dB's 99.78%). Conflating delivered Shannon throughput (258 vs 451 Mbit/s) with "service" is misleading. Furthermore, asserting that *"a better packing could do the same at zero EE cost"* is pure speculation [I] unsupported by any empirical algorithm in the repository.

2. **Overstating the multi-step inertness of Dinkelbach updates (§D.ii):**
   The audit asserts that because $\eta\cdot E$ never flips the one-step myopic argmax (0/240 probed decisions), Dinkelbach RL is inherently *"decorative"*.
   - *Flaw:* While this holds strictly for the myopic one-step decision under a $1/U$ equal-share reward, in a true multi-step MDP with non-zero discount $\gamma$, cumulative returns over trajectories where beam-lighting decisions compound could theoretically create actionable continuation value contrast in $Q_E$. The audit proves inertness for the *one-step myopic probe under this specific parameterization*, but overclaims it as an intrinsic flaw of Dinkelbach RL.

3. **Incomplete characterization of academic contribution (§I):**
   The audit dismisses the entire direction as a "negative result + baseline diagnosis" only.
   - *Flaw:* Discovering that the published IEEE baseline formulation (eq. 16 per-head max, non-spatial beam channel, unaligned handover/load weights) breaks down completely once placed on an orbital mechanics and antenna pattern simulation with physical power accounting is a substantial, publication-grade contribution to the LEO satellite communications literature. Framing it as "merely a thesis chapter" undersells the diagnostic achievement.

## 3. Verdict on the Audit

### **DEAD-PATH Verdict: SURVIVES WITH CORRECTIONS**

The audit's technical verdict that the current research direction (a per-user DQN optimizing pooled EE via equal-share energy labels and rule-based catfish demonstration replay) is a **DEAD-PATH** is **SOLID AND FULLY SURVIVES**.

The adversarial attack successfully exposed several analytical flaws:
1. **Cross-archive condition transfer:** Extrapolating $I/N \approx 5.9$ from pre-pin `sat` runs to the pinned `427e6a91…` physics without paired verification.
2. **Physical inaccuracy on beam cancellation:** Claiming beam count strictly cancels in pooled EE, which ignores pointing geometry and baseband power, and contradicts the audit's own marginal EE derivation.
3. **Selective literature critique:** Weaponizing DQfD's ablation against margin-free replay while ignoring that subsequent algorithms (R2D3, DDPGfD) deliberately dropped margin loss.
4. **Citation hallucination:** Providing an impossible May 2026 arXiv identifier (`arXiv:2605.02416v1`) for a 2024 IEEE Letters paper.
5. **Arbitrary ceiling boundaries:** Setting 3% and 10% thresholds without statistical grounding.

However, **none of these corrections rescue the proposed learner**:
- The probe proving that equal-share energy moves the argmax in **0 of 240** decisions is 100% verified.
- The pilot report (`CF3-PILOT-2026-09-11.md`) decisively proves that all learned policies (A1: 98.6M, A2: 102.0M, A3: 101.2M) remain trapped far below the simple one-line hysteresis rule `A m=2dB` (112.2M).
- The pilot's finding that uniform-random replay (A3: 101.2M) matches expert replay (A2: 102.0M) to within 0.78% proves that replay provides non-specific state coverage, not energy-efficient demonstration.
- Beating A0 is confirmed to be an artifact of dropping anti-aligned handover and load penalties that constitute 61% of A0's reward.

Therefore, proceeding with Q5 (longer training), Q6 (more catfish replay variants), or Q7 (engineering optimizations) would waste computational resources on an algorithm structurally incapable of beating a two-parameter heuristic rule.
