# Deep Research Addendum: Distilling Privileged Centralised Teachers into a Per-User EE Policy

## 執行摘要（繁體中文）

這輪研究的核心結論是：**可以保留 demonstration learning，但應該重新定義它在專案中的角色。** 目前最有文獻根據的方向，不是讓三個 scripted Catfish policy 持續把 trajectory 混進 replay，而是把未來 centralised search、exact-difference-reward oracle、sequential best-response oracle 視為具有 privileged information 的 **teacher**，再把部署時只能看到 per-user observation 的 Q-network 視為 **student**。這在文獻上屬於幾個已存在研究脈絡的交集：Learning Using Privileged Information、asymmetric learning、Centralised Training with Decentralised Execution、centralised-teacher/decentralised-student knowledge distillation，以及 reinforcement learning from demonstrations。Vapnik 與 Vashist 在 LUPI 中正式化「訓練時有額外資訊、測試時沒有」的學習範式；Pinto 等人的 asymmetric actor-critic 讓 critic 看完整 simulator state、actor 只看影像；CTDS 更直接讓 centralised teacher 用 global observation 產生 Q-values，再由只看 local observation 的 student 模仿。citeturn18search2turn18search1turn21view0

但有一個非常重要的限制：**teacher 比 student 強，不代表 teacher 的 action 可以被 student 模仿。** Weihs 等人在 ADVISOR 工作中把這件事稱為 *imitation gap*。若兩個完整 state 對 student 看起來完全一樣、但 privileged teacher 因為看到其他 users 的 same-step choices 而在兩個 state 選不同 beam，則普通 behavior cloning 或固定 DQfD margin 的最佳解，本質上只能對 teacher actions 做條件平均；它不可能恢復 student observation 中不存在的資訊。ADVISOR 的理論例子甚至顯示 privileged teacher 的 imitation 可以讓 student 變成近乎隨機，而受限資訊下真正的最優 student policy反而可以更好。citeturn17view1turn17view2

這一點和你目前的 physics audit **高度吻合**。你提供的新結果指出 consumed joules 幾乎跟 lit-beam 數量成正比，而「某 user 的 action 是否點亮新 beam」與「是否拉低 shared beam spectral efficiency」都依賴其他 users 同一步的 action。這表示 centralised EE teacher 很可能擁有 student 無法觀察的關鍵資訊。因此，**直接用 DQfD large-margin 強迫 student 選 teacher action，是三個候選機制裡風險最大的做法**。DQfD 本身在 Atari 很成功：前一百萬 environment steps 中在 42 個遊戲的 41 個優於 PDD-DQN，並在 14/42 遊戲超越最佳 demonstration；但其基本假設不是「teacher 看得到 student 看不到的 state」。citeturn6search0turn6search8

相反地，文獻最支持的是**互動式、可拒絕 teacher 的 distillation**。DAgger 解決的是 student rollout 導致的 covariate shift：讓 learner 自己走，再請 expert 標註 learner 真正遇到的 state；ADVISOR 再多處理一步，根據 student 是否有能力從自身 observation 重現 teacher behavior，自適應地降低 imitation；Nair 等人的 Q-filter 則只有在 critic 判斷 demonstration action 優於目前 policy action 時才施加 imitation。citeturn18search0turn17view0turn19academia16

對你的 **10-step、28-action、dense-reward** 問題，我因此會把優先順序排成：

> **Difference-reward RL base + DAgger-style privileged teacher queries + ADVISOR/advantage gate + TD fine-tuning**  
> **≫ unconditional DQfD large-margin**  
> **≫ competitive Catfish replay alone**

這並不是因為 DQfD 不好，而是它最強的已發表優勢主要出現在 demonstrations 幫助 exploration 的情境；你現在的 reward 是 dense、horizon 只有 10，而且你自己的 12-run pilot 已經顯示 directed static replay 只比 random-policy replay高 **0.78% pooled EE**，反而「任何 replay pool」本身就有約 **+2.6%**。這強烈暗示下一個問題已不是「再把更多 teacher transitions 塞進 buffer」，而是**如何只傳遞 student 能使用、且確實帶來 counterfactual advantage 的 teacher information**。

最重要的是，privileged-teacher distillation 並非只能保留 teacher performance 的一小部分。CTDS 在五個 SMAC scenarios 中，三種 value-decomposition backbone 的平均 student/teacher win-rate retention 約為 **74%、71%、80%**；但單一 scenario 可以接近 **98%**，也可以低到約 **6%**。也就是說，不存在可信的固定「通常保留 90%」經驗法則。performance retention 幾乎完全取決於 teacher 的額外資訊究竟是「訓練時幫助建立更好的 value representation」，還是「執行該 action 所不可缺少的隱藏變數」。citeturn23view0turn23view1

Learning by Cheating 提供另一個很重要的正面案例：直接 supervision 只有 20% success，普通 two-stage training 16%；加入 student on-policy trajectories升至64%，加入 richer privileged “white-box” supervision達96%，兩者同時使用達 **100%**。這個結果很適合你現在的情境：成功關鍵不是「teacher trajectory量更多」，而是**更完整的 teacher supervision + student自己走過的state distribution**。citeturn17view3turn17view4

所以，如果目前正在跑的 centralised EE search 確實找到明顯高於 hysteresis rule 的 headroom，我認為 demonstration mechanism **值得保留，而且有相當好的研究合理性**；但下一版不應叫「三個 Catfish replay sources」。更好的方法學敘事是：

\[
\boxed{
\text{Privileged Centralised Teachers}
\rightarrow
\text{Student-observable / advantage filtering}
\rightarrow
\text{Per-user RL student}
}
\]

而 student 的 RL credit 用 exact 或近似 **difference reward**，解決目前 equal-share energy label 幾乎無法 attribution 的問題。真正可能成為三個 Catfish 的，也不應是三個 arbitrary rule thresholds，而是三種**具有不同 privilege / coordination depth 的 teachers**，例如：

\[
T_1=\text{simultaneous unilateral exact-DR oracle}
\]

\[
T_2=\text{sequential sweep teacher}
\]

\[
T_3=\text{joint centralised search teacher}
\]

這三者從「最容易被 per-user policy 表達」到「最強但 privilege gap 最大」形成有科學意義的階層。是否最後保留三個，應由 student-observable representability、shared-state behavioral diversity與 drop-one marginal 決定，而不是預先固定三個。多教師研究也支持這種看法：AC-Teach 明確處理互相矛盾、只在部分問題上有效的 teachers；Beliaev 等人讓模型估計 demonstrator 在不同 state 的 expertise，最終在 23 個設定中的21個勝出，平均 final reward 改善約7%，最大60%；Gandhi 等人則直接證明不相容 demonstrations 是需要偵測與過濾的問題。citeturn25search4turn25search3turn25search1

最後，**我沒有找到可靠的 peer-reviewed RL 文獻支持你們目前稱為 “Catfish” 的那一套 competitive replay/ACRM 機制是一條成熟研究線**。搜尋到的 RIS “Catfish-Effect-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning...” 在作者自己的 publication list 上仍標為 **submitted**，不是已發表 peer-reviewed paper；而可查到的正式 “catfish effect” 論文主要是 population-based metaheuristics，例如 2018 年的 flower-pollination optimization，和你現在的 RL replay/competitive reward完全不同。citeturn24search1turn24search2 因此，若論文最終走 privileged teachers 路線，我會建議把 “Catfish” 留作專案內部/方法命名或與原 thesis 的 faithful comparator，而**理論定位改靠 CTDE + privileged distillation + RLfD + counterfactual credit assignment**。這會比把 novelty 建立在一條目前查不到 peer-reviewed基礎的 “Catfish literature” 上安全許多。

The strongest theoretical point for this project comes from ADVISOR. Let \(o=f(s)\) be the deployable student's observation and \(a_T(s)\) the privileged teacher's action. When several full states \(s\) collapse to the same \(o\) but \(a_T\) differs across them, cross-entropy imitation converges toward a conditional mixture of the teacher decisions rather than to the best policy constrained to \(o\). The paper explicitly calls this the **imitation gap**. citeturn17view1

That maps almost exactly onto the project's newly identified lit-beam interaction. Suppose user \(i\)'s local observation is unchanged, but in one joint state beam \(b\) is already lit by other users and in another it is not. A central teacher can rationally choose \(b\) in the first case and avoid it in the second. If the per-user observation does not reveal this distinction, no demonstration loss can reconstruct the teacher's decision function. The right target is therefore **the best student-observable policy induced by the privileged teacher**, not literal teacher-action reproduction. This is an inference from the imitation-gap theory and the project's stated physics. citeturn17view1

Learning by Cheating is correspondingly more informative than ordinary behavior cloning. Its 16%→100% jump came from combining richer teacher supervision with student on-policy data; the authors explicitly emphasize that on-policy student rollouts expose states not present on the near-perfect teacher trajectory. citeturn17view3turn17view4 For your 10-step problem, obtaining teacher labels on student-generated states is comparatively inexpensive, which makes a DAgger-like protocol unusually attractive.

+
\text{centralised privileged teacher}

The closest verified lines are CTDS, which distils full privileged Q-vectors with an MSE loss; Lin et al., which imitate a centralized controller into decentralized agents; and the newer HINT framework, which explicitly filters centralised teacher guidance that depends on information unavailable to students. citeturn22view0turn23view2turn20search1 That makes the proposed combination **plausibly novel as an integration**, but “we found no direct precedent” should not be upgraded into an absolute novelty claim without a formal systematic review.

The reason a fixed DQfD margin is risky can be stated precisely. If a privileged teacher action \(a_T\) is optimal because of hidden joint information \(z\), the large-margin term asks the student to satisfy approximately

\[
Q(o,a_T) \ge Q(o,a)+m,\qquad a\ne a_T.
\]

If the same \(o\) occurs with another hidden \(z'\) for which a different action is optimal, the training set imposes mutually inconsistent inequalities on the same student input. ADVISOR's imitation-gap results show why averaging such supervision can be worse than optimizing the restricted student policy directly. citeturn17view1

For your problem, a much safer replacement is a **gated margin**:

\[
L_{\mathrm{demo}}
=
w(o,a_T)
\,
\max\!\left(0,m + \max_{a\ne a_T}Q(o,a)-Q(o,a_T)\right),
\]

with \(w\) requiring at least two tests: teacher action is reproducible/low-ambiguity from student information, and the teacher action has positive student-relevant counterfactual advantage. This exact formula is a proposed synthesis, not a published DQfD variant; its two conceptual components are supported separately by ADVISOR's reproducibility logic and Nair et al.'s Q-filter. citeturn17view0turn19academia16

I could not verify a sufficiently precise primary-paper match for the prompt's “normalized actor-critic” reference under that exact name and therefore would **not cite it in the manuscript yet**. Likewise, I did not verify a published privileged-observation DQfD variant that would make an unconditional demonstration margin defensible.

The CTDS table is especially useful for interpreting any forthcoming centralised-search ceiling. There is **no universal retention fraction**. On `2c_vs_64zg`, the QMIX student reaches 80.6% win rate versus an 82.5% teacher, roughly 98% retention; on `MMM2`, one QPLEX student is 1.6% versus a 26.6% teacher, only about 6%. The average across five tasks lies around 71–80%, depending on backbone. citeturn23view1

That makes the right experimental question for your project not:

> “Can DQfD retain 90% of the central search?”

but:

> “How much of the central-search advantage is **measurable from the student's observation sigma-algebra**?”

A very useful intermediate oracle is exactly the one proposed in your addendum: simultaneous best response with exact difference reward. If that teacher sees only information that can be reconstructed from each user's execution observation and substantially closes the central-search gap, student distillation is promising. If the joint teacher is excellent but the observation-compatible teacher collapses, the limitation is informational, and no demonstration algorithm can reliably erase it without changing the student observation.

The directly relevant wireless literature is considerably thinner than the general MARL credit-assignment literature. The Li–Guo–MacKenzie IEEE Access paper is a real, directly matched precedent for **difference reward in wireless association**, but I could not verify its exact percentage gain from the primary web record available during this research. citeturn24search0

For your simulator, however, the computational implication can be derived directly. A classic difference reward for user \(i\),

\[
D_i
=
G(\mathbf a)
-
G(\mathbf a_{-i},a_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}),
\]

requires a realised global evaluation plus at least one counterfactual baseline evaluation per user if credit is needed only for the chosen action. If the teacher wants exact counterfactual values for all 28 actions of all users, the naive cost becomes roughly

\[
O(N A)
\]

environment evaluations per state rather than \(O(N)\). This complexity statement is an inference from the counterfactual definition, not a claimed timing result from the IEEE paper.

That cost is much less problematic for your proposal than it first appears because the exact difference reward is only a **training-time teacher/credit computation**. The deployed per-user Q-network can still execute one 28-action forward pass with no counterfactual simulator. This is exactly the broader LUPI/CTDE bargain: spend privileged compute at training time, discard it at execution. citeturn18search2turn21view0

The especially important design distinction is:

\[
\textbf{difference reward}
\neq
\textbf{teacher action label}.
\]

The teacher may be too privileged to imitate literally, while its realised counterfactual **advantage signal** can still tell the student whether a demonstrated action was beneficial. For this project, that is likely more valuable than the old equal-share \(E_u=E_{\rm sys}/U\) label.

Accordingly, a referee-facing paper should not make a claim such as “we extend the established Catfish RL framework” unless an accepted source is produced. A safer formulation is:

> “We investigate a Catfish-inspired teacher–student mechanism, motivated by prior project/thesis work, while grounding the learning mechanism in established RL-from-demonstrations and CTDE literature.”

If a faithful implementation of the original thesis exists, it can still be an experimental comparator. It simply should not carry the theoretical burden of the new method.

\[
\text{Teacher retained}
\iff
\begin{cases}
\text{non-dominated objective/constraint region},\\
\text{unique behaviour on shared student-observable states},\\
\text{positive marginal in drop-one evaluation}.
\end{cases}
\]

That criterion is a synthesis rather than a published theorem. It is, however, closely aligned with the state-dependent expertise, compatibility and contradictory-teacher findings in AC-Teach, Beliaev et al., and Gandhi et al. citeturn25search4turn25search3turn25search1

For your specific three-teacher aspiration, the more defensible decomposition is no longer C1/C2/C3 reward semantics. It is **coordination depth**:

| Candidate teacher | Privileged information | Expected student representability | Scientific role |
|---|---|---:|---|
| \(T_\mathrm{DR}\) exact unilateral difference-reward teacher | Realised joint context for evaluating one user's counterfactual | Highest | Teaches local actions whose joint-system contribution is measurable |
| \(T_\mathrm{SEQ}\) sequential-sweep teacher | Earlier users' current-step choices | Medium | Teaches conditional coordination and beam reuse/consolidation |
| \(T_\mathrm{JOINT}\) central joint-search teacher | Full simultaneous joint decision | Lowest | Supplies upper-bound solutions and only those labels that pass representability/advantage gates |

This gives three teachers a real experimental meaning. If \(T_\mathrm{JOINT}\) adds nothing after \(T_\mathrm{DR}\) and \(T_\mathrm{SEQ}\), it should be removed; if its action labels are poorly representable but its Q/advantage information helps, distil the latter rather than its argmax.

## Design implications for the current project

The literature and your pilot jointly suggest a fairly different successor from the original three-static-pool design.

The original pilot tested approximately:

\[
\text{main replay}
+
\text{15/128 static directed source rows}
\]

with no imitation loss. According to the numbers supplied in the prompt, directed demonstrations were only **+0.78%** over random-policy pools at three seeds, while any pool was about **+2.6%** over no pool. That result does **not** show that privileged-teacher distillation will fail. It shows something narrower: **static off-policy source transitions did not transfer useful directionality under the existing credit/observation contract.**

A centralised teacher changes two things simultaneously. First, it can produce substantially better labels than simple scripted rules if the ongoing central search finds headroom. Second, with realised counterfactuals it can provide a much better **credit signal** than equal-share energy labels. That gives the next experiment a legitimate causal hypothesis:

\[
\text{teacher quality}
+
\text{counterfactual credit}
+
\text{student-state correction}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{transferable EE improvement}.
\]

I would structure the next design in four stages.

**First, measure the information/representation ceiling before any DQfD run.** Generate the same central-teacher decision under many full joint states and project every sample to the deployable per-user observation. Measure conditional teacher-action entropy, top-1/top-k action predictability, and—more importantly—the realised EE regret of the best behavior-cloned student. This operationalises the imitation-gap question identified by Weihs et al. citeturn17view1turn17view2

A useful quantity is

\[
R_\mathrm{representable}
=
\frac{
J(\pi_{\mathrm{BC/teacher}},\,o_{\mathrm{student}})
-
J(\pi_{\mathrm{base}})
}{
J(\pi_{\mathrm{teacher}})
-
J(\pi_{\mathrm{base}})
}.
\]

Unlike raw action accuracy, this asks how much of the teacher's *advantage* survives the observation restriction. A 70% action classifier can be excellent if its errors are on negligible decisions; a 95% classifier can be useless if the remaining 5% are precisely the beam-lighting decisions that determine energy.

**Second, fix credit before sophisticated demonstration machinery.** Train the per-user learner with exact difference reward or a validated approximation, **without teacher imitation**, producing the new no-demonstration control. The wireless literature contains direct precedent for difference-reward resource allocation, while LUPI/CTDE justify restricting the expensive counterfactual computation to training. citeturn24search0turn18search2

**Third, make the demonstration experiment interactive.** Rather than regenerating a huge teacher-only replay buffer, let the current student create states and ask each privileged teacher what it would do there. This is the DAgger principle and directly addresses the state-distribution problem. citeturn18search0 For a 10-step horizon, the teacher-query burden should be much more manageable than in very long-horizon robotics.

**Fourth, do not turn every teacher response into a hard margin.** Keep three channels logically separate:

\[
L
=
L_{\rm TD}^{\rm difference}
+
\alpha L_{\rm value-distill}
+
\beta L_{\rm gated-demo}.
\]

`L_TD` is always active. `L_value-distill` can match teacher Q/advantage vectors where those are available, following the spirit of CTDS. `L_gated-demo` is active only when the teacher label is both representable and counterfactually advantageous, following the logic of ADVISOR and Q-filtering. citeturn22view0turn19academia16turn17view0

The most informative first factorial is consequently not RIS-Catfish versus DQfD. It is:

| Arm | Difference-reward RL | Teacher data | Imitation/distillation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `D0` | yes | none | none |
| `D1` | yes | privileged teacher, static/off-policy | replay only |
| `D2` | yes | privileged teacher on student states | DAgger-style labels / soft Q distillation |
| `D3` | yes | privileged teacher on student states | unconditional DQfD margin |
| `D4` | yes | privileged teacher on student states | **ADVISOR/Q-filter-style gated margin** |

This directly answers whether the missing ingredient was teacher quality, distribution matching, or the loss function.

Only after `D4 > D0,D1,D2,D3` with a meaningful EE margin would I add multiple teachers. Then the correct comparison is:

\[
T_1,\quad
T_1+T_2,\quad
T_1+T_2+T_3,
\]

plus drop-one arms. AC-Teach and demonstrator-expertise work strongly argue against uniform weighting when teachers have different state-specific competence. citeturn25search4turn25search3

One particularly promising alternative to action imitation is to have the joint teacher supply **soft counterfactual advantages** over all 28 actions:

\[
A_T(o,a)
=
G(a,\mathbf a_{-i})
-
G(a_{\rm ref},\mathbf a_{-i}),
\]

then regress the student toward a temperature-softened distribution such as

\[
p_T(a|o)
\propto
\exp(A_T/\tau).
\]

This resembles policy/Q distillation much more closely than DQfD's one-hot demonstration margin. Policy Distillation showed that teacher policies can be compressed and multiple teacher policies consolidated, while CTDS transfers the complete teacher action-value set rather than only one chosen action. citeturn27academia12turn22view0 For your 28-action discrete policy, a 28-dimensional privileged advantage target is cheap for the neural network even if obtaining it from the simulator is expensive.

Crucially, when two hidden joint configurations map to the same per-user observation but yield conflicting teacher advantages, averaging **Q/advantage vectors** has a clearer interpretation than imposing contradictory one-hot margins: the student approximates the expected utility conditional on the information it actually possesses. CTDS explicitly motivates its student as approximating the expectation of the teacher Q-value over the missing information. citeturn23view0 This is probably the single most relevant mathematical precedent found in this review.

## Bottom line, realistic retention, mechanism choice, and referee demands

### Is this a recognised pattern, and what should it be called?

Yes, **the constituent pattern is well recognised**, although I found no standard named method corresponding exactly to:

> privileged centralised teacher + DQfD demonstration margin + per-user decentralised student + exact difference-reward credit.

The defensible terminology is:

> **centralised-teacher / decentralised-student reinforcement learning with privileged-information distillation and counterfactual credit assignment**

or, more compactly,

> **privileged-policy distillation under CTDE**.

The relevant established families are LUPI, asymmetric learning, CTDE/CTDS, imitation learning/RLfD, and counterfactual credit assignment. citeturn18search2turn18search1turn21view0turn23view2

If you retain the “Catfish” name, I would make it a **method label**:

> “Privileged Catfish Teachers”

rather than assert that competitive Catfish replay is an established RL paradigm. The literature search did not verify that claim. citeturn24search1

### What retention fraction is realistic?

There is no defensible universal percentage.

The best directly relevant quantitative evidence I found, CTDS, has average student/teacher retention around **71–80%**, but individual tasks range from essentially full retention to catastrophic loss. citeturn23view1 LBC shows that a student can reach extremely strong performance when its sensors contain enough information and training combines rich teacher supervision with on-policy student states. citeturn17view3turn17view4 ADVISOR proves the opposite extreme: under severe privilege mismatch, literal imitation can be worse than ignoring the expert. citeturn17view0turn17view1

For this project I would therefore **not preregister “80% teacher retention” as a success criterion**. Preregister a fraction of **teacher headroom**, conditional on the student-observation ceiling:

\[
\rho
=
\frac{EE_\mathrm{student}-EE_\mathrm{baseline}}
{EE_\mathrm{teacher}-EE_\mathrm{baseline}}.
\]

Then separately report the best behavior-cloned/student-observable ceiling. If a supervised student can recover only 30% of teacher headroom, demanding 80% from RL is irrational; if supervised representability is 95% and RL retains 40%, the learning method is at fault.

### Which mechanism best fits a dense-reward, 10-step, 28-action problem?

My ranking is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{DAgger-style on-student-state teacher correction}
+
\text{adaptive/advantage gating}
}
\]

first;

\[
\boxed{
\text{soft Q/advantage distillation + TD fine-tuning}
}
\]

a very close second and possibly the best implementation;

\[
\boxed{
\text{unconditional DQfD margin}
}
\]

as a required baseline/ablation, not the preferred final mechanism;

and

\[
\boxed{
\text{Catfish competitive/static replay alone}
}
\]

last, given both the weak peer-reviewed grounding I could verify and your project's own directed-vs-random replay result.

DAgger is particularly attractive because the horizon is only 10, so student-distribution teacher querying is tractable, and because your failure mode is not primarily lack of reward discovery. DAgger was designed to correct the mismatch between expert-distribution training data and learner-distribution execution data. citeturn18search0

ADVISOR-style gating is essential if the joint teacher uses information the student lacks; HINT independently arrives at a closely related 2026 idea in cooperative MARL, explicitly filtering teacher guidance dependent on unavailable centralized observations. citeturn17view0turn20search1

DQfD should still be implemented as a clean comparator because its large-margin formulation provides a strong test:

> Does forcibly ranking the privileged teacher action above all other actions improve or damage the restricted policy?

Given the imitation-gap theory, a negative DQfD result beside a positive gated-distillation result would itself be a strong scientific result. citeturn6search0turn17view1

For the final method, my preferred loss is therefore closer to:

\[
L =
L_{\mathrm{TD,DR}}
+
\alpha_t\,w_{\mathrm{repr}}w_{\mathrm{adv}}L_{\mathrm{teacher}},
\]

where \(L_{\mathrm{teacher}}\) preferably matches a teacher Q/advantage distribution rather than only one action, and \(\alpha_t\) can decay as the student becomes competent. The exact annealing schedule is a proposed engineering choice; I did not verify a privileged-DQfD paper establishing a canonical one.

### What would a referee demand?

A strong referee is likely to ask for four distinct demonstrations of validity.

First, **prove that the teacher's advantage exists and specify its privilege**. The central search needs a fixed information class, fixed compute budget, same underlying physics and service constraints, and an honest statement that it is a training-time oracle/lower-bound approximation rather than a deployable policy. A weaker observation-compatible oracle should sit between the central teacher and per-user learner so the information gap is measurable.

Second, **measure the imitation gap explicitly**. Report teacher-action entropy conditional on student observation, behavior-cloning top-k accuracy, teacher-action disagreement among near-identical student observations, and most importantly student-observable EE regret. ADVISOR establishes that privilege size can dramatically change imitation performance. citeturn17view1turn17view2

Third, **separate credit from demonstration**. At minimum the causal ladder should include:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{old/equal-share learner},\\
&\text{difference-reward learner without teacher},\\
&+\text{teacher replay only},\\
&+\text{static DQfD margin},\\
&+\text{on-policy/DAgger teacher},\\
&+\text{gated teacher}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Otherwise an EE improvement cannot be attributed to the teacher rather than the new credit signal.

Fourth, **prove decentralised execution**. At test time the per-user network must receive no same-step other-user assignments, active-beam set, central-search result, future realised information, counterfactual outcome or teacher state unless those quantities are explicitly part of the deployment contract. CTDS and asymmetric actor-critic are useful precedents precisely because the privileged module disappears at inference. citeturn18search1turn22view0

For multiple teachers, a referee should also expect equal data/compute budgets, teacher-specific admission statistics, pairwise behavioural overlap on the same student states, individual teacher competence, one-teacher/two-teacher/three-teacher comparisons, and drop-one marginal ablations. The multiple-demonstrator literature shows why simply pooling heterogeneous expert data is not sufficient. citeturn25search3turn25search4turn25search1

For the metric, the primary endpoint should remain the declared **pooled ratio-of-sums EE**, with bits, joules, service/QoS, handovers and active beams reported separately. Any demonstration method that raises EE through service degradation must fail the non-inferiority guard.

The final decision tree is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\textbf{Central search has no material headroom}
&
\rightarrow
\text{stop assignment-only Catfish work;}\\
&
\text{expose beam activation/power/sleep controls.}
\\[6pt]
\textbf{Central search has headroom, local/DR oracle also does}
&
\rightarrow
\text{privileged-teacher distillation is highly plausible;}\\
&
\text{start with difference reward + DAgger/gated distillation.}
\\[6pt]
\textbf{Only joint central search has headroom}
&
\rightarrow
\text{measure imitation gap before training;}\\
&
\text{if large, change observation/architecture or use CTDE,}\\
&
\text{not stronger DQfD margins.}
\end{array}}
\]

Overall, the answer to the addendum's central question is therefore **yes, demonstration mechanisms can be retained—but the scientific justification has changed**. The evidence no longer supports Catfish as “special replay data that excites the learner.” It supports **privileged teachers as training-time oracles whose useful, student-representable knowledge is selectively distilled into a decentralised policy**. The two mechanisms most worth carrying forward are **interactive DAgger-style teacher querying and gated Q/advantage distillation**, with difference reward supplying the student's credit. DQfD is valuable as the hard-margin baseline; static competitive Catfish replay should not remain the default mechanism unless a later ablation unexpectedly vindicates it.
